Winter Driving Tips for New Drivers in Ontario

By Public Star Driving School · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To drive safely in an Ontario winter as a new driver, put four matching winter tires on your car (Ontario says they can shorten braking distances by as much as 25%), keep your fuel tank at least half full, carry a winter survival kit, and clear all snow and ice from your windows, lights, mirrors and roof before you leave. On the road, slow down, leave much more space, skip cruise control on slippery pavement, and never pass a working snowplow. If you skid, release the brakes and steer gently where you want to go.

Your first winter behind the wheel can feel like learning to drive all over again. Slush fills the lanes, ice forms at stop lines and snowbanks hide the corners. For G1 and G2 drivers in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa, the first big snowfall often arrives just as road tests are booked.

Winter driving is a skill like any other. This guide covers how to get your car ready, what to do before every trip, and how to drive when the roads turn slippery. The safety advice follows Ontario's official winter driving page, checked in November 2026.

How do I get my car ready for winter in Ontario?

Do this before the first snowfall. Ontario recommends:

  • A maintenance checkup so your battery, brakes, wipers and lights are ready.
  • A fuel tank at least half full. It helps reduce moisture in the fuel system and adds weight to the car.
  • An ice scraper and washer fluid rated to minus 40°C.
  • A winter survival kit that stays in the car all season.

Do I need winter tires in Ontario?

Winter tires are one of the best safety upgrades a new driver can make. Ontario says they improve traction and control in frost, snow and ice, and can shorten braking distances by as much as 25%. Some insurers also give a discount, so ask your provider.

  • Install four winter tires. Never mix different types of tires on one car.
  • Look for the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol on the sidewall.
  • All-season tires are not winter tires. They can start to lose grip at around 7°C.
  • Studded tires are not allowed in Southern Ontario for most drivers, so they are not an option in the GTA and Durham Region.
  • Check tire pressure often. Cold weather lowers it.

What should I do before driving in snow?

  1. Check the forecast. If a storm is coming and the trip can wait, delay it.
  2. Check road conditions on Ontario 511, which also shows where plows are on provincial highways.
  3. Clear the whole car: every window, your headlights and taillights, the mirrors and the roof. Roof snow can slide onto your windshield when you brake.
  4. Let the windows defog fully so you can check your blind spots.

How do new drivers drive safely on snow and ice?

Slow down and leave space

Ontario notes that many winter collisions happen because drivers are going too fast for the road. The posted limit is a maximum for a dry day, not a target in a snowstorm. It also takes longer to stop on a slippery road, so leave a much bigger gap to the car ahead and start braking earlier for lights and stop signs.

Be smooth with every control

Hard braking, quick acceleration and sudden gear changes can all cause a skid. Steer gently on curves and press the brake and gas gradually. Ontario also advises against cruise control on wet, snowy or icy pavement, because it reduces your reaction time and control.

Watch for black ice

Winter asphalt usually looks grey and white. If the road ahead looks black and shiny, it could be ice. Bridges, overpasses and shaded spots freeze first, so ease off the gas before you reach them.

See and be seen

In poor visibility or whiteouts, turn on your full lighting system so others can see you. If conditions become dangerous, pull over somewhere safe and wait.

What do I do if my car starts to skid?

  1. Take your foot off the brake.
  2. Look and steer where you want to go, not at what you are worried about hitting.
  3. Keep your steering small. Ontario warns that steering too far can make the car spin.
  4. Once the tires grip again, straighten out and slow down gently.

Most skids start when a driver brakes or turns too hard for the grip available, so speed and space are still the best prevention.

How should I drive near snowplows?

Plows and salt trucks with flashing blue lights work the highways before, during and after storms. Ontario's advice:

  • Stay well back from working snowplows and other maintenance vehicles.
  • Never pass a working snowplow. Blowing snow and the ridge a plow leaves behind can hide the road and other vehicles.
  • Be patient. Roads are plowed in sections, so you usually will not be stuck behind a plow for more than 20 to 30 minutes.

Can winter affect my G2 or G road test?

Yes. The skills examiners look for do not change, but they get harder: full stops behind the line, smooth braking and good space cushions all take more care on slush. Severe weather can also affect test schedules, so if a storm hits on test day, check drivetest.ca or contact DriveTest about your appointment.

If your test is in the winter months, practise in winter conditions first. Our guide on how to pass the G2 road test lists the habits examiners check, and the G2 automatic fails list shows mistakes that are easier to make on a slippery road, such as rolling through a stop.

Frequently asked questions

Are winter tires mandatory in Ontario?

Ontario's winter driving page recommends winter tires but does not list them as a requirement for passenger cars. They can shorten braking distances by as much as 25%, and many insurers offer a discount.

Can a G1 or G2 driver drive in snow?

Yes. Winter weather does not change the G1 or G2 rules: G1 drivers still need a fully licensed accompanying driver. Start with quiet streets in light snow before busy roads or highways.

When should I switch to winter tires?

All-season tires can start to lose grip at around 7°C, so many drivers switch once daytime temperatures regularly drop to about that level, before the first snowfall.

How much space should I leave in winter?

Leave enough room that you could stop gently if the car ahead stopped suddenly. On ice, that can be several times your normal gap.
