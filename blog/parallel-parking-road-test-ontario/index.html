How to Parallel Park for Your Road Test in Ontario

By Public Star Driving School · November 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Parallel parking is part of the Ontario G2 road test (the G1 exit test), not the G test. Find a space about one and a half times your car's length, stop beside the car in front with your rear bumpers lined up, then reverse slowly with the wheel turned fully toward the curb, straighten once you see the other car's rear corner, and turn fully toward the road to finish parallel. Check your mirrors and blind spot before you start, keep it slow, and never touch the curb.

Parallel parking is the manoeuvre students worry about most before a G2 road test. The good news is that it follows a set method, and the Ontario Driver's Handbook spells it out. Once you know the reference points and practise them, it becomes routine. This guide walks through the steps, the checks examiners look for, and the mistakes that cost marks.

Is parallel parking on the Ontario road test?

Yes, on the first road test. Ontario's licensing page lists parallel parking among the skills checked on the G2 road test (the G1 exit test), along with 3-point turns, stopping, lane changes and driving in residential neighbourhoods.

The G road test (the G2 exit test) currently does not include parallel parking, roadside stops, 3-point turns or residential driving, because those are already covered on the G2 test. If you have passed your G2, you can focus on highway and expressway skills instead. Our G2 vs G road test guide explains the difference in detail.

Step by step: the MTO parallel parking method

These steps follow the official handbook. Practise them in this order until you do them without thinking.

  1. Find the right space. Look for a gap about one and a half times the length of your car, on the right side of the road in the direction of traffic.
  2. Signal and check behind you. Turn on your right signal early, check your mirrors, and slow down gently so drivers behind know you are stopping.
  3. Stop alongside the car in front of the space. Pull up parallel to it, leaving a gap between the two cars (the handbook's road test section says at least 60 centimetres). Stop when your rear bumper lines up with that car's rear bumper.
  4. Look all around, then reverse slowly. Check your mirrors and both blind spots, shift into reverse, and turn the steering wheel fully toward the curb as you start to back up.
  5. Straighten the wheels. When you can see the outside rear corner of the car in front of the space, straighten your wheels and keep reversing slowly.
  6. Turn toward the road to finish. Turn the steering wheel fully toward the road so the car swings in line with the curb.
  7. Adjust if needed. If you are not parallel, drive forward a little to straighten up. Do not hit the curb or the cars around you.
  8. Secure the car. Set the parking brake and put the car in park.

When the examiner asks you to move on, signal left, check your mirror and blind spot, and pull out only when there is a safe gap.

What the examiner watches during parallel parking

Car control matters, but observation matters just as much. On test day the examiner is checking that you:

What they checkWhat it looks like
SignallingRight signal on before you stop beside the other car
ObservationMirror and blind spot checks before you reverse and before you pull out
SpeedSlow, controlled reversing, no sudden jerks
PositionClose to the curb and parallel, without touching it
SmoothnessA small number of corrections, not repeated back and forth
SafetyYielding to pedestrians, cyclists and cars while you manoeuvre

A common surprise: many students lose marks on parallel parking for not looking, not for a crooked finish. Turn your head and look out the rear window while you reverse, not only at the mirrors or a backup camera.

Common parallel parking mistakes

These are the mistakes our instructors correct most often in the weeks before a road test:

  • Reversing too fast. Speed makes every small error bigger. Creep back at walking pace or slower.
  • Turning the wheel too late or too early. Use the reference point: the outside rear corner of the car in front. That is your cue to straighten.
  • Hitting or climbing the curb. A light touch costs marks. Mounting the curb is treated as a serious error, as we explain in our G2 automatic fails guide.
  • Forgetting the blind spot. Cyclists and pedestrians can appear beside you while you reverse.
  • Starting too far from the parked car. A big sideways gap leaves you far from the curb at the end.
  • Giving up and trying again from scratch. It is usually better to make one small forward correction than to pull out and start over.

How to practise parallel parking

Practice turns the steps into muscle memory. A few ideas that work well:

  • Start in an empty lot. Use two pylons, recycling bins or cardboard boxes to mark a space. You can make mistakes without risk.
  • Move to a quiet residential street. Choose a road with little traffic and one or two parked cars, at a time when it is calm.
  • Repeat the same reference points every time. Line up your bumpers, wheel fully toward the curb, straighten at the corner, wheel fully toward the road.
  • Practise in the car you will test in. Every car has a different length and turning circle. If you test in your instructor's car, practise in it too.
  • Practise both sides on one-way streets. Ontario allows parking on both sides of a one-way road unless signs say otherwise, so be ready for either.

Most students need several focused sessions before parallel parking feels easy. Our guide on how many driving lessons you need before your G2 can help you plan. The other residential tasks on the same test are covered in our three-point turn and roadside stop guide.

Parking rules to remember

Parallel parking well is only half of it. You also need to choose a legal spot. The handbook says, among other rules, that you should not park:

  • Within 3 metres of a fire hydrant
  • Within 9 metres of an intersection, or 15 metres if it has traffic lights
  • Within 15 metres of the nearest rail of a level railway crossing
  • In a way that blocks a sidewalk, crosswalk, driveway or another parked vehicle

On a hill, turn your front wheels toward the curb when facing downhill, and away from the curb (toward the road) when facing uphill with a curb. That way the curb stops the car if it rolls.

Frequently asked questions

Is parallel parking on the G test in Ontario?

Not currently. Ontario says the G road test does not include parallel parking, roadside stops or 3-point turns, because they are already covered on the G2 road test.

How big a space do I need to parallel park?

The Ontario Driver's Handbook recommends a space about one and a half times the length of your vehicle.

Can I fail the G2 test for bad parallel parking?

Small errors such as one extra correction are marked and added up. A dangerous action, like reversing without looking or hitting another car, can end the test.

Can I use a backup camera during the road test?

A camera can help, but do not rely on it alone. Examiners expect you to turn and look behind you and check your blind spots while reversing.

How close to the curb should I be?

The handbook does not give an exact number for parallel parking, but you should end up close to the curb and parallel to it without touching it. For a roadside stop, the handbook says to stop no more than about 30 centimetres from the curb.
