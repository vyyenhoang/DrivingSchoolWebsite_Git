G2 Road Test Automatic Fails: What Ends Your Test in Ontario

By Public Star Driving School · November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

DriveTest does not publish an official list of G2 automatic fails, but examiners can stop the test if your driving puts safety at risk, so one dangerous action such as rolling a stop, failing to yield, an unsafe lane change or making the examiner step in usually ends it. Before you even drive, a car problem like a dead brake light, cracked windshield or active dashcam gets the test declared "out-of-order" and you lose 50% of your road test fee. After a fail you generally wait at least 10 days to retest.

Most students worry about parallel parking, but that is rarely what ends a G2 road test. Tests are usually lost in two ways: something about the car or the paperwork stops the test before it starts, or one dangerous action during the drive ends it early. This guide covers both, using DriveTest's own rules and what our instructors see on test day.

Is there an official G2 automatic fail list?

No. DriveTest does not publish a list of "automatic fail" mistakes. What it does say is that the examiner scores you against set criteria on a scoresheet, and that the examiner may stop or refuse to start the test if:

  • Your vehicle is not in good working order
  • You show any signs of impairment from alcohol or drugs
  • Your driving skills are not good enough to finish the test without risking safety

That last point is what people mean by an automatic fail. Minor errors are marked and added up. A single action that creates real danger can fail you by itself, however well the rest of the drive went.

Driving mistakes that usually fail you on the spot

These are the actions our instructors see examiners treat as dangerous. One of them is normally enough to end the test:

  • Rolling through a stop sign. Stop completely behind the line. If you cannot see, creep forward after the full stop.
  • Not yielding the right of way to pedestrians in a crosswalk, cyclists, or a car that has the right of way at a four-way stop.
  • An unsafe lane change. Changing lanes without a mirror and shoulder check, or forcing another driver to brake.
  • Running a red light, or entering on an amber you could have stopped for safely.
  • Speeding in a school zone or community safety zone, or driving well over the limit anywhere.
  • The examiner having to intervene, by warning you or reaching for the wheel to prevent a collision.
  • Hitting the curb hard or mounting it, or touching another vehicle or object.
  • Turning left into oncoming traffic without a safe gap.
  • Ignoring a school bus with its red lights flashing.

Notice the pattern: almost every item is about observation and judgment, not car control. Our G2 road test guide explains how to build the scanning habits that prevent these errors.

Car problems that cancel your test before it starts

Before you drive, the examiner walks around your car and may ask you to test the signals and brake lights. If the vehicle does not meet MTO standards, the test is declared out-of-order. DriveTest lists many reasons. The ones that catch G2 students most often are:

An out-of-order test is not a fail on your record, but it still costs you. You lose 50% of your road test fee. The other half stays as a credit, and you pay the missing half to book again. This applies whether the car is yours, borrowed from family, rented, or a driving instructor's.

Other things that end your test before you drive

Some problems have nothing to do with how you drive:

  • Showing up with no vehicle. DriveTest does not provide cars.
  • Not wearing glasses or contacts when your licence requires them.
  • Refusing to wear a seatbelt.
  • Booking through a BDE course when your driving school has not certified you by test day.
  • Booking the wrong test class, such as a G2 test when you meant to book a G.

Most of these also cost 50% of the fee, so check everything the week before.

What to bring so nothing goes wrong

DriveTest asks you to arrive at least 30 minutes early and bring:

  1. A vehicle in good working order
  2. Glasses or contact lenses, if you need them to drive
  3. Enough gas for the test
  4. Your current driver's licence
  5. A printout of your road test confirmation email

Plan a ride home in case the test does not go your way. Remember that a G1 driver needs an accompanying driver, and that person cannot ride along during the test itself.

If you need to cancel, Ontario says to do it at least 48 hours ahead to avoid a cancellation fee. Check current road test fees on drivetest.ca.

What happens after a failed G2 test

At the end, the examiner gives you a short verbal summary, and your scoresheet shows where you lost marks. If you took the test on a tablet, you can download your results online within 15 days.

You can take as many road tests as you need while your licence is valid. DriveTest says you generally must wait at least 10 days between tests, and you pay the road test fee again. If your G1 expires before you pass, you have to start the process over.

Use the scoresheet. If you failed for a rolling stop, spend your next lessons on stops and intersections, not on parking you already do well.

Frequently asked questions

Is a rolling stop an automatic fail on the G2 test?

It is one of the most common reasons a test ends. DriveTest does not publish an automatic fail list, but a stop that is not complete is treated as a safety risk. Always stop fully behind the line.

Can you fail the G2 test before you start driving?

Yes, in effect. If your car has a broken brake light, a cracked windshield, a recording dashcam or another defect, the test is declared out-of-order and you lose 50% of your road test fee.

How many minor mistakes can you make on the G2 test?

There is no published number. Minor errors are marked on the scoresheet and added up, so a few small slips will not fail you on their own, but one dangerous action can.

How long do you wait to retake the G2 test after failing?

DriveTest says you generally must wait at least 10 days between road tests, and you pay the road test fee again.

Can I use my parent's car for the G2 road test?

Yes. The car can be owned, borrowed or rented, but it must pass the examiner's vehicle check. Check every item in the out-of-order table the week before, or book a package that includes your instructor's car.
