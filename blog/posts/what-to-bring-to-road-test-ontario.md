Road test day is stressful enough without realizing in the parking lot that your licence is at home or the car has a burnt-out brake light. The good news is that the list of things to bring is short. This guide covers exactly what DriveTest asks for, what to check on the car, how the day runs, and the small extras that make it easier.

## What does DriveTest say to bring to a road test?

DriveTest's road test page lists six things to bring on test day:

1. **A vehicle in good working order.** DriveTest does not provide cars.
2. **Glasses or contact lenses,** if you need them to drive.
3. **Enough gas** in the vehicle to take the test.
4. **Your current driver's licence.**
5. **A printout of your road test confirmation email.**
6. **Money for test fees,** only if you are hoping for a standby road test appointment.

That is the whole official list. Everything else in this guide is about making sure each item is ready.

## Your licence and confirmation email

Bring the driver's licence you are testing on: your G1 if you are taking the G2 test, or your G2 if you are taking the G test. Check the expiry date the week before. DriveTest says novice drivers may not book a road test after their licence expiry date, and you can only keep taking tests while your licence is valid.

Print the confirmation email you got when you booked, since DriveTest lists it as an item to bring.

## Glasses or contact lenses

If you need corrective lenses to drive, wear them for the test. If you wear contacts, a spare pair of glasses is a smart backup.

## What the car needs on test day

You must bring a Class G vehicle of the right class to the centre. DriveTest says it can be owned, borrowed or rented, and it does not need to be a driving instructor's vehicle. Low-speed vehicles cannot be used.

Before the test starts, the examiner walks around the car looking for damage, safety hazards and anything that would stop the test. If the car does not meet the minimum standards, the test can be declared "out of order" and you lose 50% of your prepaid road test fee.

Check these the day before, with a helper standing outside the car:

| Check | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Lights | Headlights, brake lights and all four turn signals work |
| Horn and brakes | Horn sounds, brakes and parking brake work |
| Windshield and mirrors | No crack in your line of sight, mirrors intact |
| Seatbelts | Both front belts buckle and release |
| Dashboard | No warning lights suggesting the car is unsafe |
| Fuel | Enough gas to finish the whole test without stopping |
| Documents | Ownership and proof of insurance in the car |

### Turn off cameras and recording devices

DriveTest does not allow in-vehicle recording devices during road tests, including cell phones, dashboard cameras and action cameras. They must be disabled. If the car has a camera that cannot be turned off, you must confirm out loud that it is not recording the passenger compartment or pointed at the examiner. If you cannot confirm that, the test is declared out of order.

Put your phone on silent and out of reach before the examiner gets in.

## Who can come with you

No pets and no passengers other than the examiner are allowed in the car during the test. Friends, family and your driving instructor can wait in the designated safe areas. DriveTest says you can, with consent, use a translator or driving instructor during check-in and at the debrief after the test.

Plan your trip there and back:

- **Taking the G2 test on a G1?** G1 rules mean you must always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience, so you cannot drive yourself to or from the centre alone.
- **Taking the G test on a G2?** DriveTest suggests making plans for getting home if you are not successful, either an accompanying driver or other transportation.

## How the day runs

1. **Arrive at least 30 minutes early.** DriveTest asks for this, and arriving late means losing your prepaid fee.
2. **Park and check in inside.** Most centres have a self-serve kiosk; see a customer service agent if yours does not.
3. **Go back to the car and wait.** The examiner comes to you. If no one arrives within 30 minutes of your appointment time, go back inside to Counter A.
4. **Listen to the instructions.** The examiner explains the test before you start and is not allowed to coach you, so ask any questions first.
5. **Drive the test.** The G2 test usually takes about 20 minutes and the G test about 30 minutes.
6. **Get your summary.** The examiner gives you a short verbal summary. If you pass, go back inside to apply your results and get a temporary licence.

Road tests run in any weather unless you are told otherwise. If the weather looks bad, check DriveTest's Service Disruptions page on the morning of your test.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to bring my booking confirmation to the road test?

DriveTest lists a printout of your road test confirmation email as one of the items to bring. It is a good idea to have it with you at check-in.

### Do I need to pay at the DriveTest centre on test day?

Not for a regular booked test. Road test fees are prepaid when you book. DriveTest only says to bring money for test fees if you are hoping for a standby appointment.

### Can I use my phone or dashcam during the road test?

No. Recording devices, including phones, dashcams and action cameras, are not permitted and must be disabled. If a built-in camera cannot be turned off, you must confirm it is not recording the inside of the car or the examiner.

### Can my instructor or a family member ride along?

No. Only you and the examiner can be in the car. Others wait in a designated area, and an instructor or translator can help, with consent, at check-in and at the debrief.
