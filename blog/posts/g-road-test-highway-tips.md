The G road test is the last step to your full Ontario licence. It is different from the G2 test: less about parking and quiet streets, much more about driving at speed on major roads and expressways. If you are comfortable on the highway, it is very passable. If you are not, it is the part that catches most people off guard. Here is what the test covers and a checklist our instructors use to get students ready.

## When you can take the G road test

You can book the G road test once you have held your G2 licence for **12 months**. Ontario gives you up to **five years** to finish the whole graduated licensing process. If you do not get your full G in that time, you have to start over, so do not leave the G test until the last few months.

## What the G road test checks

According to ontario.ca, the G test covers more advanced driving skills and still includes the fundamentals:

- Driving on major roads and expressways, including merging on and off
- Keeping an appropriate speed and space around your car
- Signalling, turns, curves and lane changes
- Intersections
- Driving in business areas

Right now, the standard G test **does not include** parallel parking, the roadside stop, the three-point turn or driving in residential neighbourhoods. Those skills were already tested on your G2. Ontario has said this applies until further notice, so check ontario.ca before your test in case it changes.

That means almost all of your score comes from moving traffic. The highway section is where you need to be at your best.

## The Declaration of Highway Driving Experience

Before the test starts, you must complete and sign a **Declaration of Highway Driving Experience**. It asks how many times in the three months before your test you drove on a freeway or a highway with a speed limit of at least 80 km/h, and how long those trips were.

This matters: if you do not have enough highway experience, the examiner must declare the test "out of order" and cancel it, and you **lose 50 per cent of your prepaid road test fee**. Plan real highway trips in the months before your test, not just the week before.

## Highway driving checklist for the G test

Practise each of these until you do it without thinking.

### Entering the highway

1. **Look ahead on the ramp.** Find the gap you want to merge into while you are still on the ramp.
2. **Build speed.** Use the acceleration lane to reach the speed of traffic. Merging too slowly is one of the most common highway mistakes.
3. **Signal early** so drivers in the right lane know you are coming.
4. **Mirror, then blind spot.** Turn your head to check over your left shoulder before you move over.
5. **Merge smoothly** into the gap. Do not stop at the end of the ramp unless traffic leaves you no choice.

### Driving on the highway

- **Keep a safe following distance.** Leave a gap of at least two seconds, more in rain or heavy traffic.
- **Hold a steady speed** at or near the limit. Watch for changes in posted limits and construction zones.
- **Scan your mirrors** every few seconds so you always know who is beside and behind you.
- **Stay out of other drivers' blind spots** and do not sit beside a truck longer than you need to.
- **Keep right except to pass.**

### Changing lanes at speed

1. Check your mirror.
2. Signal.
3. Shoulder check in the direction you are moving.
4. Move over gradually, keeping your speed.
5. Cancel your signal.

Examiners can only score what they can see, so move your head clearly for every shoulder check.

### Exiting the highway

- **Know your exit early.** Move into the right lane well before the exit.
- **Signal before the exit lane begins.**
- **Slow down in the exit lane**, not on the highway itself.
- **Watch the ramp speed.** Exit ramps often curve sharply, so brake before the curve, not in it.

## City driving still counts

Away from the highway, the G test still looks at turns, intersections and business areas. The habits that got you through your G2 still apply: full stops behind the line, left, right and left again at every intersection, and safe gaps before turning left. Our [G2 road test guide](/blog/how-to-pass-g2-road-test/) covers these basics and the mistakes that cause an automatic fail.

## What to bring on test day

- Your G2 licence
- A car in good working order that is plated and insured
- Glasses or contact lenses if you need them to drive
- Payment for any test fee you have not paid yet

Arrive at least 30 minutes early. Only you and the examiner may be in the car, so friends, relatives and instructors cannot ride along. Driving aids such as cruise control, lane monitoring and back cameras may not be used during the test. For the current G road test fee and booking, check [drivetest.ca](https://drivetest.ca).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is highway driving on the G road test in Ontario?

Yes. The G test includes driving on major roads and expressways, including merging on and off. You must also sign a Declaration of Highway Driving Experience before the test.

### Is parallel parking on the G road test?

Not at the moment. Ontario says the standard G test does not include parallel parking, the roadside stop, the three-point turn or residential driving until further notice, because those were covered on the G2 test.

### How long do I need my G2 before the G test?

You need to hold your G2 licence for 12 months. You have up to five years in total to finish the whole licensing process.

### What happens if I do not have enough highway experience?

The examiner must cancel the test as "out of order" and you lose 50 per cent of your prepaid road test fee. Drive on highways of 80 km/h or more in the three months before your test.
