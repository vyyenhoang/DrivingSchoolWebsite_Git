Moving to Ontario comes with a long to-do list, and getting the right driver's licence is near the top for most newcomers. The good news: your driving experience back home still counts for something. How much it counts depends on where your licence comes from. This guide walks through the rules as ontario.ca explains them, plus what we see helps newcomers pass their road tests in Ajax, Scarborough and across Durham Region.

## How long can you drive in Ontario on a foreign licence?

According to ontario.ca, you can use a valid licence from another province, state or country for **60 days**. After 60 days, you need to switch to an Ontario driver's licence.

Sixty days goes by quickly when you are also finding a home, a job and a school for your kids. Start the licence process in your first few weeks, especially if you will need to book road tests.

## Which countries can exchange their licence in Ontario?

Ontario lets you exchange an out-of-province licence if you are relocating from:

- any Canadian province or territory
- the Canadian Armed Forces
- a U.S. state (U.S. territories and protectorates are not included)
- Australia, Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Denmark, France, Germany, Great Britain, Hungary, the Isle of Man, Japan, South Korea, Kosovo, New Zealand, Northern Ireland, the Republic of Ireland, Switzerland, Taiwan or Ukraine

Even from one of these places, the exchange is not automatic. Ontario says the process depends on where you were licensed, your licence class and your driving experience, and that you might still need to take a knowledge test or road test. Check the exchange details on drivetest.ca for your exact situation before you go.

## What if your country is not on the exchange list?

Most newcomers to the Greater Toronto Area come from countries without an exchange agreement, including India, Pakistan, the Philippines, China, Iran, Nigeria and many others. If that is you, ontario.ca lists these steps:

1. Pass the **vision test** and the **knowledge test** at a DriveTest centre.
2. Pass the **G2 road test**.
3. Wait **at least 12 months** before you attempt the G road test.
4. Pass the **G road test** to get your full G licence.

Since **July 1, 2026**, you can get credit for **up to one year** of driving experience by showing a valid, original foreign driver's licence and an authentication document. Ask DriveTest how that credit applies to your timeline when you apply.

For a full picture of how the levels fit together, see our [G1 to G licence timeline](/blog/g1-to-g-licence-timeline-ontario/).

## What is a letter of authentication?

To get credit for your foreign experience, you need a letter of authentication. Ontario says it must be:

- an official letter from the foreign government or agency that issued your licence
- a confirmation that your licence is authentic
- written in English or French

Request this letter early. Depending on the country, it can take weeks to arrive, and you may need family back home to help.

## Documents to bring to DriveTest

You apply in person at a DriveTest centre or at ServiceOntario Bay and College in Toronto. Bring:

| Document | Notes from ontario.ca |
| --- | --- |
| Identity documents | Originals showing your legal name and date of birth |
| Foreign driver's licence | Original and valid, with a visible expiry date |
| Translation | Needed if the licence is not in English or French |
| Letter of authentication | Needed to get credit for driving experience |
| Proof of driving experience | Original supporting documents in English or French |

**Translations:** the translation must be done by a certified translator and dated within six months of your application. Ontario accepts translations from non-Canadian embassies, consulates or high commissioner's offices, but not other translators from the country that issued the licence. Translators must provide proof of their credentials.

Since May 11, 2026, Class G applicants also declare that Ontario is their primary place of residence and that their presence in Canada is legal.

DriveTest charges fees for the licence application, the knowledge test and each road test. Check the current list on drivetest.ca before you go, since we cannot confirm amounts here.

## Why experienced drivers still fail the Ontario road test

This is the part that surprises many newcomers. You may have driven safely for years, yet Ontario examiners mark specific habits that are normal elsewhere. The ones we see most often:

- **Rolling stops.** Ontario expects a full stop behind the line at every stop sign.
- **Shoulder checks.** Examiners watch for a head turn before every lane change, merge and turn from the curb. Mirrors alone are not enough.
- **Right turns on red.** They are allowed, but only after a complete stop and when it is safe.
- **School buses and pedestrians.** The rules around stopped school buses and crosswalks are strict.
- **Speed in school zones and residential streets.** Posted limits change often, and examiners notice.

None of this is hard to fix, but old habits are automatic. A few lessons with an instructor who knows the test routes usually clears them up. Our [G2 automatic fails list](/blog/g2-road-test-automatic-fails/) covers the mistakes that end a test early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive in Ontario with an international driving permit?

Ontario's exchange page says you can drive on a valid licence from another country for 60 days, then you need an Ontario licence. It does not describe the international driving permit, so check with DriveTest or your insurer about your situation.

### Is India on Ontario's licence exchange list?

No. India is not on the list ontario.ca gives. Drivers from India take the vision test, knowledge test and G2 road test, and can get credit for up to one year of experience with a valid licence and a letter of authentication.

### Do I need to take the knowledge test if I have driven for years?

If your licence is from a country without an exchange agreement, yes. Ontario lists the vision test and knowledge test as the first steps. Drivers from exchange countries may or may not need tests, depending on their circumstances.

### Where can I get my licence translated?

Ontario accepts translations from a certified translator, including non-Canadian embassies, consulates and high commissioner's offices. The translation must be dated within six months of your application.
