Metro East is one of the busiest DriveTest centres for drivers from Scarborough, North York and East York. Lots of our students book their G2 or G test there because it is close to home. This guide covers the practical side: where it is, when road tests run, what it costs, what happens after you check in, and how to prepare for driving in that part of the city.

## Where is the Metro East DriveTest centre?

DriveTest lists the centre as **Toronto Metro East** at:

**Victoria Terrace Plaza, 1448 Lawrence Ave E, Unit 15, Toronto, M4A 2V6**

It sits in a plaza on Lawrence Avenue East, so you park in the plaza lot and walk in to check in. If you are coming from Scarborough, Pickering or Ajax, give yourself extra time: Lawrence and the roads that feed into it can be slow at rush hour, and you do not want to start your test already stressed about being late.

## What happens on test day at Metro East

DriveTest's process is the same at every centre, but knowing it ahead of time takes a lot of the nerves away.

1. **Arrive at least 30 minutes early.** DriveTest asks for this at every centre.
2. **Park and go inside to check in.** Most centres have a self-serve kiosk; if not, see a customer service agent.
3. **Go back to your car and wait.** The examiner comes to you. If no examiner arrives within 30 minutes of your appointment time, DriveTest says to go back inside to Counter A.
4. **Vehicle check.** Before you drive, the examiner checks that the car is in good working order. Lights, signals, horn, brakes and the windshield all matter.
5. **The drive.** DriveTest says the G2 test usually takes about 20 minutes.
6. **Results.** The examiner gives you a short summary at the end and you get a scoresheet.

A few rules catch people out every week:

- **No passengers.** Your instructor, friends and family cannot ride along. Only you and the examiner are in the car.
- **No recording.** Phone, dashboard and action cameras must be turned off. If a camera cannot be turned off, you must confirm out loud that it is not recording the inside of the car or pointed at the examiner, or the test is declared out-of-order.
- **No driving aids.** Ontario's handbook says electronic aids such as automatic parallel parking, lane monitoring, cruise control and back cameras may not be used during the test. Practise parking with your mirrors and shoulder checks.
- **Plan your ride home.** If you are taking your G2 test on a G1, you cannot drive home alone if you fail, so bring a fully licensed driver or arrange another way home.

## How to prepare for the roads around Metro East

DriveTest does not publish its test routes, and examiners can take you anywhere nearby. So instead of memorising one route, get comfortable with the kinds of roads you will meet in this area:

- **Busy multi-lane arterials.** Lawrence Avenue East and the main roads near the plaza carry heavy traffic, buses and lots of turning cars. Practise lane changes with a mirror check, signal and shoulder check every single time.
- **Left turns at busy lights.** Waiting in the intersection for a gap, then turning into the correct lane, is a skill examiners watch closely. Do not rush a left turn because someone behind you is impatient.
- **Residential streets.** Expect quiet side streets with stop signs, parked cars and pedestrians. Come to a full stop behind the line, look both ways, and keep your speed down.
- **Leaving and entering the plaza.** Your test starts and ends in a parking lot. Drive slowly, watch for pedestrians, and signal when you pull out onto the road.
- **Parking and reversing.** The G2 test checks parallel parking and reversing, so practise in quiet streets until it feels routine.

If you are taking the G test instead, add expressway practice. Our [G road test highway tips](/blog/g-road-test-highway-tips/) cover merging, lane changes at speed and exits, and our [G2 vs G road test guide](/blog/g2-vs-g-road-test/) explains how the two tests differ.

## Should you take your test at Metro East or somewhere else?

There is no official ranking of "easy" DriveTest centres, and we will not make one up. The better question is which centre's roads you can practise on most. If you live in Scarborough, North York or East York, Metro East is often the most convenient choice. Students in the east end sometimes prefer Port Union, and students in Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa often book [Oshawa](/blog/oshawa-drivetest-tips/). Pick the centre near where you can get real practice, then book early, since test dates can fill up.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the address of Metro East DriveTest?

Victoria Terrace Plaza, 1448 Lawrence Ave E, Unit 15, Toronto, M4A 2V6, according to DriveTest's centre list.

### Does Metro East DriveTest do G road tests?

Yes. DriveTest lists Metro East as offering G2 and G road tests, plus M2 and M motorcycle tests.

### What time are road tests at Metro East?

DriveTest lists road test appointments Monday to Saturday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Check the service disruptions page on the day of your test.

### Can my driving instructor ride with me during the test?

No. Only you and the examiner can be in the car. Your instructor can drive you there, warm you up, and wait for you.

### Is Metro East harder than other DriveTest centres?

There is no official data showing one centre is harder. Every centre uses the same scoring standards. What matters most is practising on roads like the ones around the centre you choose.
