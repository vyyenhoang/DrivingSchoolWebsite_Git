North York is the busiest of the six cities our instructors teach in. It has some of the heaviest traffic in Ontario, a stretch of Highway 401 that many new drivers find intimidating, and plenty of quiet side streets where you can learn the basics first. This guide explains how lessons in North York work, which roads help you prepare for your road test and what it all costs.

## What does a driving lesson in North York look like?

Every lesson is one-on-one, so the full hour is yours. Your instructor picks you up from your home, school or workplace in North York, whether that is near York University, a subway station on Yonge Street or a quiet street in Don Mills, and plans each lesson around what you need next.

Your first lesson usually includes:

- **Getting set up:** seat, mirrors and steering wheel position before you move.
- **A quick assessment:** starts, stops, turns and lane position on quiet streets.
- **Honest feedback:** what you already do well and which habits would cost marks on a road test.
- **A plan:** a realistic estimate of how many lessons you need and what each one will cover.

Each lesson after that builds on the last one. In North York, that usually means extra time on the skills busy city traffic demands: scanning for pedestrians and cyclists, choosing the right lane early and making left turns at large intersections without rushing.

## What licence do you need to start lessons in North York?

You need a **G1 licence**, which you get by passing a vision test and the written knowledge test at a DriveTest centre. Once you have it, you can start lessons with a certified driving instructor right away.

While you hold a G1 in Ontario, you must:

- Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
- Not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
- Not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways such as the 401.
- Always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience, and who is the only other person in the front seat.

There is one important exception. When you drive with a driving instructor certified in Ontario, you can drive on any road. For North York drivers that matters a lot, because Highway 401, Highway 404 and the Don Valley Parkway all run through or beside the city. A lesson is the only way to practise on them while you hold a G1.

## Local roads worth practising on in North York

North York packs almost every kind of driving into a small area. Lessons usually move through it in stages:

| Stage | Type of road | What you practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Residential streets in neighbourhoods like Willowdale, Bayview Village and Newtonbrook | Steering control, full stops, speed control, scanning for children and pedestrians |
| Building up | Main roads such as Bathurst Street, Bayview Avenue and Leslie Street | Left turns at lights, lane changes, following distance |
| Busier traffic | Yonge Street, Sheppard Avenue and Finch Avenue | Heavy traffic, buses, plaza entrances, pedestrians and cyclists |
| G test prep | Highway 401, Highway 404 and the Don Valley Parkway | Merging, exiting and changing lanes at highway speed |

DriveTest does not publish its test routes, so memorizing streets is not the goal. The goal is to feel calm on every type of road, so nothing on test day feels new. A driver who can handle Yonge and Sheppard at rush hour is usually well prepared for any test route.

## Driving on Highway 401 through North York

The 401 through North York has express and collector lanes, frequent exits and heavy traffic for much of the day. For a G road test, you need to merge, change lanes and exit smoothly at highway speed. We start highway practice only once you are comfortable on busy city roads, and we usually begin outside rush hour.

The skills we focus on:

1. **Building speed on the ramp** so you merge at the speed of traffic, not below it.
2. **Checking mirrors and blind spots** before every lane change, every time.
3. **Planning exits early** so you are in the correct lane well before the ramp.
4. **Keeping a safe following distance** even when other drivers do not.

## How many lessons will you need?

Ontario does not set a minimum number of lessons. It does set waiting periods: you must hold your G1 for 12 months before your G2 road test, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course. After that, you hold your G2 for 12 months before you can take the G road test.

As a rough guide for North York students:

1. **Confident drivers** who practise often with family usually need 2 to 3 hours of test-focused lessons.
2. **Drivers who still hesitate** at left turns, lane changes or busy intersections usually need 4 to 5 hours.
3. **Complete beginners** do best with 10 hours or a full BDE course.

## Tips for getting the most out of your lessons

- **Practise between lessons.** Even 20 minutes on quiet streets helps new skills stick.
- **Learn to expect the unexpected.** Pedestrians, cyclists and buses pulling out are part of every North York drive, so keep scanning.
- **Ask questions.** Your instructor would rather explain a right of way rule twice than watch you guess on test day.
- **Know the common mistakes.** Read our list of [G2 road test automatic fails](/blog/g2-road-test-automatic-fails/) before you book.

Only with a certified driving instructor. G1 drivers cannot use 400-series highways with a family member, but when you drive with an instructor certified in Ontario, you can drive on any road.
