The G1 knowledge test is the first step to driving in Ontario, and it is the one step you can fully control with study at home. There is no car, no examiner beside you and no time limit. If you know the handbook, you will pass. This guide explains how the test works, what it costs, what to bring and how to study so you pass on the first try.

## What is the G1 knowledge test?

To apply for a driver's licence in Ontario, you must be at least 16 years old, live in Ontario, pass a vision test and pass a knowledge test about the rules of the road and traffic signs. Once you pass both, you get your G1 licence and can start practising with an experienced driver.

DriveTest describes the knowledge test like this:

- **Multiple choice**, created by the Ministry of Transportation (MTO)
- **Two or three sections**, for example one on signs and one on rules of the road
- **Not timed**: you can take as long as you need, until the office closes
- **Usually 20 to 30 minutes** to finish
- **80% total score** needed to pass
- **Marked on the spot**, so you get your result right away
- On paper at every centre, and on a computer at some centres

## How much does the G1 test cost?

DriveTest sets the fees, which include tax. These are the current amounts on the [DriveTest fees page](https://drivetest.ca/tests/fees/):

| Fee | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Class G1 licence package (knowledge test, first G2 road test and five-year licence) | $159.75 |
| Knowledge test, per extra attempt | $16.00 |
| Class G2 road test (retest, or if bought separately) | $53.75 |
| Class G road test | $91.25 |

You pay the G1 package when you apply. Because it already includes your first G2 road test, you do not pay again when you book that test later. DriveTest accepts cash, Visa, Mastercard, debit and a few other methods listed on its fees page.

## Do I need an appointment for the G1 test?

No. Knowledge tests are offered at every DriveTest centre without an appointment. DriveTest recommends arriving at least one hour before the office closes so you have time to register and finish the test. Ontario also lists the ServiceOntario location at Bay and College in downtown Toronto, but that one is by appointment only.

If you need a verbal test or an interpreter, you do need to make an appointment at a DriveTest centre first.

## What to bring to the G1 test

Bring **original identification that shows your legal name and date of birth**. Ontario lists accepted documents for Canadian citizens, permanent residents and temporary residents. Common examples are:

- A passport (Canadian or foreign)
- A Permanent Resident Card
- A study permit or work permit
- An Ontario Photo Card

See the full list on Ontario's [get a G driver's licence page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/get-g-drivers-licence-new-drivers) before you go. Also bring your glasses or contacts if you wear them, since you take the vision test at the same visit, and a way to pay the fee.

## Can I take the G1 test in my language?

Yes, in many cases. The computer and paper versions of the Class G1 knowledge test are offered in 32 languages, including English, French, Arabic, Bengali, Cantonese, Farsi, Hindi, Korean, Punjabi, Russian, Spanish, Tamil, Ukrainian and Urdu. The computer kiosks also have an audio option with free disposable headphones, and you can use your own wired headphones with a 3.5 mm plug. Tell the agent before you go into the test room if you want the audio version.

## How to study for the G1 knowledge test

Every question comes from the **Official MTO Driver's Handbook**. You can read it online for free, or buy a printed copy at a DriveTest centre (cash only) or from many stores. DriveTest recommends studying from a printed copy because the online version is partial.

A simple two week plan works for most people:

1. **Days 1 to 3: signs.** Learn the shapes and colours first (octagon means stop, triangle means yield, diamond means warning), then the meaning of each sign. Signs are the easiest marks to lock in.
2. **Days 4 to 8: rules of the road.** Read the chapters on intersections, right of way, school buses, emergency vehicles, passing, parking and speed limits. Take notes on any rule that has a number attached.
3. **Days 9 to 10: G1 and G2 rules.** Know the conditions on your own licence: zero blood alcohol, no driving between midnight and 5 a.m., no 400-series highways, and an accompanying driver with at least four years of experience. Our [G2 licence rules guide](/blog/g2-licence-rules-ontario/) covers the next level.
4. **Days 11 to 12: penalties.** Review demerit points and suspensions for new drivers. Our guide to [G1 and G2 demerit points](/blog/g1-g2-demerit-points-ontario/) puts them in one place.
5. **Days 13 to 14: practice questions.** Ontario publishes sample G1 questions on the MTO website. Answer them without the book, then go back to every chapter where you got one wrong.

### Tips for test day

- **Read every answer before choosing.** Many questions have two answers that look right; one is more complete.
- **Watch for numbers.** Distances, blood alcohol levels and hours are where most people slip.
- **Use the time.** The test is not timed, so there is no reason to rush.
- **Do not bring help.** Anyone caught cheating or disrupting others gets an unsuccessful mark, loses the fee and must wait until the next day to try again.

## What happens if I fail the G1 test?

You can pay $16.00 and try again. DriveTest says test results are valid for one year, and if you retry within that year you only need to redo the sections that did not meet MTO standards. So if you passed signs but not rules, you only study and retake rules.

## What comes after you pass?

You walk out with a G1 licence and start the graduated licensing process. In most cases you practise for 12 months before your G2 road test, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved Beginner Driver Education (BDE) course. You have five years to finish the whole process. Our [G1 to G timeline guide](/blog/g1-to-g-licence-timeline-ontario/) walks through every step, and our guide on [whether BDE is worth it](/blog/is-bde-course-worth-it/) explains the shorter wait and what the course includes.

With a G1, you can drive on any road, including the 401, when you are with an Ontario certified driving instructor. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are on the G1 test in Ontario?

DriveTest does not publish a fixed number of questions. It says the test is multiple choice, has two or three sections such as signs and rules of the road, and usually takes 20 to 30 minutes. You need a total score of at least 80% to pass.

### Is the G1 test timed?

No. You can take as long as you need until the DriveTest centre closes. That is why DriveTest suggests arriving at least an hour before closing.

### Can I take the G1 test online at home?

No. The knowledge test is proctored at a DriveTest centre. You take it in person on paper or, at some centres, on a computer.

### How old do you have to be to take the G1 test?

You must be at least 16 years old and a resident of Ontario to apply for a driver's licence.

### Do I have to pay the full $159.75 again if I fail?

No. The G1 licence package is paid once. Each extra knowledge test attempt is $16.00, and within one year you only retake the sections you did not pass.
