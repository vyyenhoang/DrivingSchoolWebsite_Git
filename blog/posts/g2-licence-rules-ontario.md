Passing your G2 road test is a big moment: for the first time, you can drive without an experienced driver beside you. But a G2 is still a novice licence, and Ontario keeps several rules in place until you pass your G test. This guide explains every G2 restriction, what happens if you break one, and how to get through the 12 months safely. It is written for new drivers in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa, but the rules apply across Ontario.

All of the rules below come from [ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/get-g-drivers-licence-new-drivers) and are current as of November 2026. Rules can change, so check the official pages if you are unsure.

## What can you do with a G2 licence in Ontario?

Compared with a G1, a G2 gives you a lot more freedom. Ontario says G2 drivers can drive:

- without another experienced driver in the car,
- on all Ontario roads, including 400-series highways and high-speed expressways,
- at any time of the day, in most cases.

That is why the G2 stage is the best time to build real highway experience before your G test. Our [G road test highway tips](/blog/g-road-test-highway-tips/) explain what to practise.

## G2 restrictions at a glance

| Rule | Who it applies to |
| --- | --- |
| Zero blood alcohol level | All G2 drivers |
| No drugs, including cannabis, in your system | All G2 drivers |
| Only as many passengers as working seatbelts | All G2 drivers |
| Midnight to 5 a.m.: only one passenger aged 19 or under (first six months) | G2 drivers aged 19 or under |
| Midnight to 5 a.m.: up to three passengers aged 19 or under (after six months) | G2 drivers aged 19 or under |
| No hand-held phone use while driving | All drivers, with tougher penalties for novices |

## Zero tolerance for alcohol and drugs

Ontario's zero tolerance law says you cannot have any alcohol in your system if you hold a G1, G2, M1 or M2 licence, at any age. The same applies to all drivers aged 21 or under, even with a full G. The rule also covers cannabis and other drugs: Ontario says young and novice drivers must have no alcohol and no drugs in their system when they drive.

Ontario lists these penalties for young and novice drivers who break zero tolerance, on top of any other impaired driving charges:

| Offence | Immediate suspension | Penalty | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First time | 7 days | $250 | 8 hour education course |
| Second time | 14 days | $350 | 16 hour treatment program |
| Third time | 30 days | $450 | 16 hour treatment program and a 6 month ignition interlock |

If you are convicted, the fine can be $60 to $1,000, and your licence can be suspended for a further 30 or 90 days or cancelled. A reinstatement fee also applies each time a licence is suspended. The simple answer: if you have had anything at all, do not drive.

## G2 passenger rules

Every G2 driver must carry only as many passengers as there are working seatbelts. No exceptions.

If you are **19 or under**, there is an extra rule between midnight and 5 a.m.:

1. **First six months on your G2:** only one passenger aged 19 or under.
2. **After six months:** up to three passengers aged 19 or under.

These late-night limits do not apply if a fully licensed driver with at least four years of experience is in the car, or if your passengers are immediate family members, such as a brother or sister.

## Phones and distracted driving on a G2

Ontario's distracted driving law applies to everyone, but novice drivers face harsher consequences. You cannot hold a phone or other device while driving, including when you are stopped in traffic or at a red light. You can use a hands-free device only to turn it on and off, and a mounted phone only to touch a button to make, answer or end a call.

The fine is $615 if settled out of court, and more if you are summoned to court. Novice drivers do not get demerit points for this offence. Instead, Ontario says G1 and G2 drivers face:

- a 30 day licence suspension for a first conviction,
- a 90 day licence suspension for a second conviction,
- cancellation of your licence and removal from the graduated licensing system for a third conviction, which means starting over.

Set your GPS and music before you pull out, and put your phone out of reach.

## Demerit points for G2 drivers

Novice drivers reach suspension much sooner than fully licensed drivers. According to Ontario's [demerit point page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/understanding-demerit-points):

| Demerit points | What happens to a novice driver |
| --- | --- |
| 2 to 5 | Warning letter |
| 6 to 8 | Second warning letter |
| 9 or more | 60 day licence suspension |

Points stay on your record for two years from the offence date. A fully licensed driver, by comparison, is not suspended until 15 points.

## Escalating penalties for novice drivers

Ontario also has escalating penalties for G1 and G2 drivers who are convicted of breaking graduated licensing rules, or of a Highway Traffic Act offence worth four or more demerit points:

- **First offence:** 30 day suspension.
- **Second offence:** 90 day suspension.
- **Third offence:** you lose your novice licence and must start again, taking all tests and paying all fees. You also lose any time credit, including the time saved from a driver education course.

Losing your G2 can set you back many months, so these rules are worth taking seriously.

## How long do you keep a G2?

You hold your G2 for at least 12 months before you can take the G road test. Ontario gives you up to five years to finish the whole process from G1 to G, or you have to start over. ## Tips for a smooth G2 year

- **Drive often, not just on weekends.** The 12 months are meant for experience, so use them.
- **Get highway practice early.** Start with quiet times on the 401 or 412 before rush hour.
- **Keep a clean record.** Even two demerit points trigger a warning letter.
- **Plan late-night trips.** If you are 19 or under, count passengers before you leave after midnight.
- **Book a refresher before your G test.** A lesson or two helps you fix habits that crept in.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive on the highway with a G2 in Ontario?

Yes. G2 drivers can drive on all Ontario roads, including 400-series highways and high-speed expressways, without another driver in the car.

### Can a G2 driver drink any alcohol before driving?

No. G2 drivers must keep a zero blood alcohol level and have no drugs, including cannabis, in their system.

### How many passengers can I have with a G2?

Only as many as there are working seatbelts. If you are 19 or under, between midnight and 5 a.m. you can carry one passenger aged 19 or under in your first six months, and up to three after that, unless an experienced driver is with you or the passengers are immediate family.

### What happens if I get caught on my phone with a G2?

You face a $615 fine if settled out of court and a 30 day suspension for a first conviction, 90 days for a second, and cancellation of your licence for a third.

### How many demerit points can a G2 driver have?

A warning letter comes at 2 points, a second at 6, and a 60 day suspension at 9 or more.
