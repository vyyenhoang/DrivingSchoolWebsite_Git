Public Star Driving School is based in Ajax, so this is the town our instructors know best. If you are a new driver here, or you are getting ready for your G2 or G road test, this guide explains what a lesson actually looks like, which local roads are worth practising on, where Ajax drivers usually take their road test, and what it costs.

## What happens in your first driving lesson in Ajax?

Your first lesson is mostly about finding out where you are. Your instructor picks you up from your home, school or workplace and starts on quiet residential streets before moving to busier roads once you are comfortable.

During that first hour, expect to:

- **Adjust the car properly:** seat, mirrors and steering wheel, before you move.
- **Show what you can already do:** smooth starts and stops, turns, and lane position.
- **Get honest feedback:** a short list of what you do well and what needs work.
- **Agree on a plan:** how many lessons you likely need and what each one will cover.

If you have never driven before, the first lesson stays in quiet areas. If you already practise with family, your instructor will move quickly to the things that cost marks on a road test.

## What do you need before you can book a lesson?

You need at least a **G1 licence**. Ontario gives you a G1 after you pass the vision test and the written knowledge test at a DriveTest centre. With a G1, you can drive with a certified driving instructor, and lessons are a good way to start safely.

Remember that G1 drivers must:

- Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
- Not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
- Not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways, unless you are with a driving instructor certified in Ontario.
- Always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience.

That third rule matters in Ajax, because Highway 401 runs right through town. With a certified instructor, you can practise highway merging before you are allowed to do it with family.

## Local roads worth practising on in Ajax

Ajax has a good mix of the roads you will see on a road test: quiet crescents, school zones, busy arterial roads and highway ramps. Lessons usually build up through them in this order:

| Stage | Type of road | What you practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Residential streets and crescents | Steering, full stops, speed control, watching for pedestrians |
| Building up | Main roads such as Harwood Avenue, Westney Road and Salem Road | Lane changes, left turns at lights, following distance |
| Busier traffic | Kingston Road and Bayly Street | Multi-lane driving, plazas, buses and turning traffic |
| G test prep | Highway 401 | Merging, exiting, lane changes at highway speed |

DriveTest does not publish its road test routes, so the goal is not to memorize streets. It is to get comfortable with every type of road, so nothing on test day surprises you.

## How many lessons will you need?

It depends on how much you have already driven. Ontario does not require a minimum number of lessons. You must hold your G1 for 12 months before your G2 road test, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course.

As a rough guide:

1. **Confident drivers** who practise often with family usually need 2 to 3 hours, focused on test skills.
2. **Drivers who still hesitate** at left turns or lane changes usually need 4 to 5 hours.
3. **Complete beginners** are better off with 10 hours or a full BDE course.

Our guide on [how many driving lessons you need before your G2](/blog/how-many-driving-lessons-before-g2/) goes into more detail, including the signs that you are ready to book.

## Should you take the BDE course?

A Ministry-approved BDE course lets you take your G2 road test after 8 months instead of 12, and most insurers offer a discount for new drivers who complete it. Only with a certified driving instructor. G1 drivers cannot use 400-series highways with a family member, but highway practice is allowed during a lesson with an instructor certified in Ontario.
