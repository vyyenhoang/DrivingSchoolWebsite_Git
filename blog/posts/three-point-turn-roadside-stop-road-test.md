The three-point turn and the roadside stop are two of the shortest tasks on the G2 road test, but they are packed with checks that are easy to forget under pressure. The examiner usually asks for them on a quiet residential street, often back to back. This guide follows the official MTO Driver's Handbook so you know exactly what to do and what the examiner is looking for.

## Are the three-point turn and roadside stop on the Ontario road test?

Yes, on the **G2 road test (the G1 exit test)**. They are part of the residential driving section, along with parallel parking.

The **G road test (the G2 exit test)** does not include them at the moment. The handbook says that until further notice the G test will not include parallel parking, roadside stops, 3-point turns or driving in residential neighbourhoods, because those are already covered on the G2 test. Our [G2 vs G road test guide](/blog/g2-vs-g-road-test/) explains what each test covers.

## How to do a roadside stop, step by step

The examiner will say something like "pull over and stop behind the next parked car" or "stop at the side of the road when it is safe." Here is the handbook method, split into the three parts the examiner marks.

### 1. The approach

- **Check traffic before you slow down.** Look in your mirrors and scan for signs to make sure stopping there is legal. The handbook says a 150 metre gap in traffic in both directions gives you enough space to do it safely.
- **Check your right blind spot** if a cyclist or pedestrian could pass you on the right.
- **Signal before you slow down.** If there are driveways or side roads between you and your stopping point with cars waiting to pull out, wait until you pass them so nobody thinks you are turning.
- **Slow down steadily.** No sudden braking.
- **Stop parallel to the curb, not more than about 30 centimetres away.** If there is no curb, stop as far off the travelled part of the road as you can. Do not block a driveway or other traffic.

### 2. The stop

- **Turn off your signal and turn on your hazard lights.**
- **Put the car in park and set the parking brake** (automatic transmission).

### 3. Resuming

- **Release the parking brake** and select drive.
- **Turn off your hazard lights and turn on your left signal.**
- **Check your mirrors and your left blind spot** just before you pull away.
- **Accelerate smoothly** back to normal traffic speed, then cancel your signal.

Forgetting to switch from hazards back to the left signal is one of the most common small errors we see. Say it out loud in practice: "hazards off, signal on, mirror, shoulder, go."

## How to do a three-point turn, step by step

A three-point turn is how you turn around on a narrow road. On the test the examiner will ask you to pull over first, so the turn starts from a roadside stop on the right side of the road.

1. **Pull over on the right.** Use the roadside stop approach above and stop parallel to the curb, no more than 30 centimetres away.
2. **Signal left.** Turn on your left signal before you start the turn.
3. **Check all around.** Look in your mirrors and over your left shoulder. Wait until the way is clear in both directions, or traffic has stopped to let you turn.
4. **Forward, wheel sharply left.** Move slowly and smoothly across the road toward the far curb, turning the wheel fully to the left.
5. **Stop before the far curb.** Do not touch it. Shift into reverse.
6. **Check traffic again.** The handbook says to check in both directions every time you stop during the turn.
7. **Reverse, wheel sharply right.** Look behind you and back up slowly with the wheel turned fully right until the car faces the new direction. Do not reverse over the edge or shoulder of the road or into the curb.
8. **Stop, shift to drive, check and go.** Check your mirrors, then accelerate smoothly to normal speed.

The handbook's key rule for the test: **use the whole road and reverse only once.** If you need a fourth or fifth point, it usually means you started too far from the curb or turned the wheel too slowly.

## What the examiner watches

| Task | What the examiner checks |
| --- | --- |
| Roadside stop approach | Mirror check, legal spot, signal timing, steady slowing |
| Roadside stop position | Parallel, about 30 cm or less from the curb, not blocking anything |
| After stopping | Signal off, hazards on, park and parking brake |
| Resuming | Hazards off, left signal, mirrors and left blind spot |
| Three-point turn | Signal, checks at every stop, full road width, one reverse only |
| Car control | Slow, smooth movement, no curb contact |

Most marks on these tasks are lost for missed observation, not for the turn itself. Turn your head on every check so the examiner can see you looking. Our [G2 automatic fails guide](/blog/g2-road-test-automatic-fails/) covers the mistakes that can end a test.

## Where you should not make a three-point turn

You will not get to choose the spot on the test, but in everyday driving the handbook says not to make a three-point turn:

- On a curve in the road
- On or near a railway crossing or a hilltop
- Near a bridge or tunnel that blocks your view

The handbook also says the simplest and safest way to turn around is often to drive around the block.

## Common mistakes and how to fix them

- **Stopping too far from the curb.** More than about 30 centimetres away costs marks and leaves you with less room for the turn. Practise judging the gap with a friend outside the car.
- **Leaving the signal on after stopping.** Switch to hazard lights as soon as you stop.
- **Checking only the mirrors.** Shoulder checks are required before pulling out and before the turn.
- **Rushing the steering.** Turn the wheel fully while the car creeps forward, so you reach the far side at the right angle.
- **Touching the curb.** Stop a little short. A gap is fine; contact is not.
- **Forgetting the parking brake.** It is part of the stop, even for a short pause.

## How to practise

Find a quiet, wide residential street with good sightlines in both directions. Practise the roadside stop until the sequence is automatic, then add the three-point turn from that stop. Practise in the same car you will test in, because turning circles differ. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is the three-point turn on the G test in Ontario?

Not currently. The handbook says the G road test will not include 3-point turns, roadside stops or parallel parking until further notice, because they are covered on the G2 test.

### How close to the curb should I stop for a roadside stop?

Parallel to the curb and not more than about 30 centimetres away. If there is no curb, stop as far off the travelled part of the road as possible.

### Do I use hazard lights during a roadside stop?

Yes. Once you have stopped, turn off your signal and turn on your hazard lights. Before you pull away, turn the hazards off and signal left.

### What if I cannot finish the three-point turn in three moves?

The handbook says to use the whole road and reverse only once, so extra moves can cost marks. Starting close to the right curb and turning the wheel fully helps you finish in three.

### Should I check my blind spot during the turn?

Yes. Check your mirrors and blind spot before you start, and check traffic in both directions each time you stop during the turn.
