Demerit points are one of the most misunderstood parts of Ontario's licensing system, especially for new drivers. If you hold a G1 or G2, the limits are much lower than for someone with a full G, and a single serious ticket can put your licence at risk. This guide explains how the system works for novice drivers, how many points common offences carry, and what happens at each level.

It is written for students in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa, but the rules apply across Ontario. Everything below comes from Ontario's [understanding demerit points](https://www.ontario.ca/page/understanding-demerit-points) page, checked in November 2026. Rules can change, so check that page if you are unsure.

## How do demerit points work in Ontario?

You do not start with a bank of points that you "lose". Every driver starts at zero, and points are **added** to your record when you are convicted of certain driving offences. A few key facts:

- Points are added only on **conviction**, not when you get the ticket.
- Points stay on your record for **two years from the offence date**.
- The number of points depends on the offence, from 2 up to 7.
- Convictions in other Canadian provinces and territories, New York State and Michigan can also add points to your Ontario record.

If you collect enough points, the Ministry of Transportation will warn you first and then suspend your licence.

## Who counts as a novice driver?

Ontario treats you as a novice, or new, driver if you hold a **G1, G2, M1, M2, M1-L or M2-L** licence. Novice drivers face different, stricter consequences for demerit points than drivers with a full G or M licence. If you are not sure where you are in the process, our [G1 to G licence timeline](/blog/g1-to-g-licence-timeline-ontario/) walks through each stage.

## Demerit point limits: novice vs full licence

| Points on your record | G1 or G2 (novice) | Full G licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 to 5 | Warning letter | No action |
| 6 to 8 | Second warning letter | Warning letter |
| 9 to 14 | 60 day suspension | Second warning letter |
| 15 or more | 60 day suspension | 30 day suspension |

The difference is big. A novice driver is suspended at 9 points, while a fully licensed driver is not suspended until 15. Two mid-level speeding tickets can be enough to take a G2 driver off the road.

## What happens at 9 points as a G1 or G2 driver?

If you reach 9 or more points as a novice driver, your licence is suspended for **60 days**. The Ministry of Transportation sends you a letter with the date the suspension starts and instructions to surrender your licence.

You can surrender your licence in person at any ServiceOntario centre or by mail to the Ministry. You **cannot** surrender a suspended licence at a DriveTest centre. If you do not surrender it, you can lose your licence for up to two years.

### After the suspension ends

When the 60 days are over, you may need to retake your vision, written and road tests. Once you pass and your licence is reinstated:

- your points are reduced to **4** if you hold a novice licence,
- those points stay on your record for two years,
- new points could bring you back for an interview,
- reaching too many points again leads to a further **6 month** suspension.

In other words, after one suspension you have very little room for mistakes.

## Escalating penalties: the rule many new drivers miss

Demerit points are only half the picture. Ontario also has an **escalating penalties** program for novice drivers. It can apply if you are:

1. convicted of breaking a graduated licensing rule, such as the zero alcohol rule,
2. convicted of a Highway Traffic Act offence worth **4 or more** demerit points, such as careless driving or speeding by 30 km/h or more, or
3. given a court-ordered suspension for an offence that would otherwise carry 4 or more points.

The penalties grow with each offence:

- **First offence:** 30 day suspension.
- **Second offence:** 90 day suspension.
- **Third offence:** you lose your novice licence and must start over, taking all tests and paying all fees again. You also lose any time discount, such as the time saved from a driver education course, any credited time and any fees you paid.

This means a single 4 point ticket can suspend a G2 driver for 30 days, even though 4 points alone would only bring a warning letter. If you took a [beginner driver education course](/blog/is-bde-course-worth-it/) to shorten your G1 wait, a third offence wipes out that benefit.

## How to keep your record clean as a new driver

Most demerit points come from a handful of habits. These are the ones we work on most in lessons:

- **Watch your speed on arterial roads.** Roads like Kingston Road, Sheppard Avenue and Highway 2 change speed limits often. Speeding by 30 km/h or more is 4 points and can trigger an escalating penalty.
- **Come to a full stop.** Rolling through a stop sign is 3 points and a common road test fail. Our [defensive driving tips](/blog/defensive-driving-tips-ontario/) cover how to scan intersections properly.
- **Leave space.** Following too closely is 4 points. Keep at least a two second gap, more on the highway or in bad weather.
- **Yield at crossovers.** Pedestrian crossovers carry 4 points if you fail to stop. Stop and wait until people have completely crossed the road.
- **Signal every time.** It is only 2 points, but it adds up quickly and is easy to fix.
- **Move over for emergency vehicles and tow trucks.** Slow down and change lanes when it is safe.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many demerit points can a G2 driver have in Ontario?

A G2 driver gets a warning letter at 2 to 5 points, a second warning letter at 6 to 8 points, and a 60 day suspension at 9 or more points.

### How long do demerit points stay on your record in Ontario?

Demerit points stay on your driving record for two years from the date of the offence.

### Do G1 drivers get demerit points?

Yes. G1 drivers are novice drivers, so the same limits apply as for G2 drivers: warning letters at 2 and 6 points and a 60 day suspension at 9 points. Escalating penalties also apply.

### Can one speeding ticket suspend a G2 licence?

It can. Speeding by 30 km/h or more carries at least 4 points, and a novice driver convicted of an offence worth 4 or more points can receive a 30 day suspension for a first offence under the escalating penalties program.

### Do out-of-province tickets add demerit points in Ontario?

Yes, for certain offences. Convictions in other Canadian provinces and territories, New York State and Michigan for offences such as speeding, running a stop sign or careless driving add points as if the offence happened in Ontario.
