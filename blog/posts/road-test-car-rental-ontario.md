You booked your road test, and now you need a car. Maybe the family car is not available, has a warning light on, or you just do not want to test in an unfamiliar vehicle. This guide covers what Ontario requires from the car, what happens if it is not ready, your options, and what a driving school car costs in Scarborough and Durham Region.

## Can you use a rented car for your road test in Ontario?

Yes. Ontario does not require you to own the car you take your test in. The Official MTO Driver's Handbook says you must bring "the appropriate vehicle" to your road test, and that it must be fit, in proper working condition, plated and insured.

So a family car, a rental or a driving school car can all work, as long as it meets those standards and you are allowed and insured to drive it. The handbook's advice on choosing a driving school even lists "use of vehicle for road test" as something a school's contract should spell out.

## What the car must have on test day

Before you drive anywhere, the examiner checks the car. Here is what to confirm the day before:

- **Valid plates and insurance.** The car must be plated and insured, and you should have the ownership and insurance documents in the car.
- **Working lights and signals.** Headlights, brake lights and every turn signal, front and back.
- **Working horn and brakes.** Including the parking brake.
- **A clean, uncracked view.** A windshield crack in your line of sight, or a broken mirror, can be a problem.
- **No dashboard warning lights** that suggest the car is not safe to drive.
- **Working seatbelts** for both you and the examiner.
- **Enough fuel** to finish the test without stopping.

## What happens if the car is not ready

This is the part that catches people out. Ontario's handbook says that if your vehicle does not meet ministry standards for a road test, the examiner declares the test **out-of-order**. When that happens:

1. You lose 50% of your road test fee.
2. The other 50% stays on your driving record as a credit.
3. To book again, you pay the missing 50% first.

An out-of-order test is not a fail, but you still pay for it and wait for a new date. If you are worried about your car, the handbook says to contact the DriveTest centre before your test.

## Rules that apply no matter whose car it is

A few road test rules from Ontario's handbook affect how you plan the car:

- **No passengers.** Only you and the examiner can be in the car. Driving instructors, friends, relatives and translators cannot ride along.
- **No driving aids.** Automatic parallel parking, lane monitoring, cruise control and back cameras may not be used during the test. If you have learned to park with a back camera, practise with mirrors and shoulder checks before test day.
- **Arrive at least 30 minutes early.** The handbook's checklist says to arrive at least 30 minutes before your appointment.
- **Plan your ride home.** If you are taking the G2 test on a G1, a fully licensed driver needs to come with you, and if you are taking the G test on a G2, be ready to get home another way in case you do not pass.
- **Cancel early if you need to.** If you cancel or reschedule with less than 48 hours notice, or miss the test, your prepaid road test fee is not refunded.

## Your options for a road test car

| Option | Good points | Things to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family or friend's car | Free, and you may already practise in it | It must pass the vehicle check, the owner's insurance must cover you, and a fully licensed driver may need to come with you |
| Rental company car | Newer car in good condition | Many rental companies have their own age and licence rules, so ask whether a G1 or G2 driver can drive it and how insurance works. Newer cars often have driving aids you will need to switch off or ignore |
| Driving school car | Checked before the test, instructor brings it, warm-up drive included | Book early, since popular test dates and times fill up |

A regular rental can work, but you may be driving an unfamiliar car on the most important drive of the year.

## Tips if you use a rented or borrowed car

1. **Drive it once or twice before the test** to get used to the brakes and turning circle.
2. **Find every control before you arrive**: signals, hazards, horn, wipers, defroster and parking brake.
3. **Check every light the night before** with a helper standing outside the car.
4. **Bring the documents**: ownership and proof of insurance in the car, plus your licence and glasses or contacts if you need them to drive.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a driving school car for my road test in Ontario?

Yes. A driving school car is a normal choice and Ontario's handbook lists use of a vehicle for the road test as something driving schools may offer. The car must be in proper working condition, plated and insured, like any other test car.

### Can my driving instructor sit in the car during the test?

No. Only you and the examiner can be in the car. Your instructor can drive you to the centre, warm you up and wait for you.

### What happens if my rental car fails the vehicle check?

The examiner declares the test out-of-order. You lose 50% of your road test fee, the other half stays as a credit, and you pay the missing half to book a new test.

### Can I use the back camera in my car during the test?

No. Ontario's handbook says electronic aids such as back cameras, automatic parallel parking, lane monitoring and cruise control may not be used during the road test.
