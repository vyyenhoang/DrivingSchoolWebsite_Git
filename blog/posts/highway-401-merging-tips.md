The 401 is the busiest highway most new drivers in the east end will ever use, and the on-ramp is where nerves usually peak. The good news: merging is a skill with clear steps, and once you have done it a few times with someone calm beside you, it stops feeling like a leap of faith. This guide walks through each part of a 401 merge, plus lane changes, trucks and exits.

## Can you drive on the 401 with a G1 or G2?

With a **G1**, ontario.ca says you must not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways such as the 401, the QEW or the Gardiner. There is one exception: if you are driving with a driving instructor certified in Ontario, you can drive on any road. That is why many G1 students get their first highway practice in a lesson car.

With a **G2**, you can drive on all Ontario roads, including the 401. The usual G2 conditions still apply, such as a zero blood alcohol level. Our [G2 licence rules guide](/blog/g2-licence-rules-ontario/) covers the full list.

## How to merge onto the 401: step by step

Ontario's Driver's Handbook describes a freeway entrance in two parts: the **entrance ramp** and the **acceleration lane**. Treat them as two separate jobs.

### On the ramp: plan your merge

1. **Look ahead early.** As soon as you can see the highway, start watching the right lane for a gap you could fit into.
2. **Check your mirrors and left blind spot** while you are still on the ramp. You want a picture of traffic before you need to act.
3. **Watch the ramp itself.** Many 401 ramps curve, so keep your speed sensible until the ramp straightens out.

### In the acceleration lane: match the speed

1. **Signal left** so drivers in the right lane know you are coming.
2. **Accelerate firmly.** The handbook says to use the acceleration lane to bring your speed up to the speed of traffic before merging. Merging at 70 km/h into traffic doing 100 km/h is one of the most common new driver mistakes.
3. **Shoulder check again** right before you move over.
4. **Move over smoothly** into your gap, then cancel your signal.

### If there is no gap

Adjust your speed to line up with a gap rather than stopping. Speed up a little to get ahead of a car, or ease off to slip in behind it. Stopping at the end of an acceleration lane is a last resort, because you then have to merge from a standstill.

A few freeway entrances join on the **left**, which puts you straight into the fastest lane. The handbook says to build your speed more quickly on those ramps.

## Tips that make 401 merges easier

- **Commit once you have chosen a gap.** Hesitation, speeding up and slowing down again, confuses the drivers around you.
- **Do not cut anyone off.** The handbook notes it is dangerous and illegal for a slower vehicle to cut in front of a faster one.
- **Be the courteous driver too.** When you are already on the highway, move left if it is safe so merging drivers have room.
- **Avoid rush hour at first.** Your first few merges are easier in lighter traffic, such as a weekend morning.
- **Keep both hands on the wheel.** Most lane changes should be done without taking a hand off the wheel.

## Driving along the 401 once you are on

Merging is only the start. Once you are on, the goal is a steady, predictable drive.

| Habit | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Following distance | At least two seconds behind the vehicle ahead, more in rain, snow or behind trucks |
| Scanning | Look 15 to 20 seconds ahead and check mirrors every five seconds or so |
| Lane choice | Keep right and use the left lanes to pass, then move back |
| Speed | Hold a steady speed at or below the posted limit, slower in bad weather or construction |
| Blind spots | Do not sit in other drivers' blind spots, especially beside large trucks |

To check your gap, pick a sign or pole ahead. When the car in front passes it, count "one thousand and one, one thousand and two." If you reach the marker before you finish, you are too close.

### Collector and express lanes

Through Toronto and Scarborough, the 401 splits into **collector** and **express** lanes. The collectors have more exits; the express lanes have fewer exits and are better for longer trips. You can only switch between them at marked transfers, so read the overhead signs early and know which side your exit is on before you get on the highway. If you are not sure, the collectors are usually the safer choice for a new driver.

### Sharing the road with trucks

The 401 carries a lot of transport trucks. They block more of your view and have large blind spots to the sides and back, so pass them steadily instead of sitting beside them. On many freeways with three or more lanes each way, trucks cannot use the far left lane, so expect them in the centre and right lanes.

## Exiting the 401 safely

The handbook describes three parts to an exit: a **deceleration lane**, an **exit ramp** and an intersection at the end.

1. **Move to the right lane early.** Exit signs come well before the exit, so you have time.
2. **Signal into the deceleration lane, but do not slow down on the highway.**
3. **Slow down once you are in the deceleration lane**, down to the speed posted for the ramp.
4. **Check your speedometer.** After highway driving, 60 km/h can feel like 30. The handbook calls this speed adaptation, or velocitization.
5. **Be ready to stop** at the end of the ramp.

If you miss your exit, never stop or reverse on the highway. Take the next one and turn around.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive on the 401 with my G1?

Only with a driving instructor certified in Ontario. Otherwise, ontario.ca says G1 drivers must not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways such as the 401.

### How fast should I be going when I merge onto the 401?

As close as you can to the speed of traffic in the right lane, without going over the limit. Use the whole acceleration lane to build speed before you move over.

### What should I do if no one lets me merge?

Adjust your speed to line up with a gap, by speeding up to get ahead of a car or easing off to fall in behind one. Avoid stopping at the end of the acceleration lane unless there is truly no other choice.

### Should new drivers use the collector or express lanes?

The collector lanes are usually easier for new drivers because they have more exits and give you more chances to get off. Read the overhead signs early so you are in the right set of lanes for your exit.

### How much following distance do I need on the highway?

At least two seconds behind the vehicle ahead in good conditions. Leave more space in bad weather and behind large trucks or motorcycles.
