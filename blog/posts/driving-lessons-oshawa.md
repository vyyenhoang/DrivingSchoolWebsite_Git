Oshawa is one of the six cities our instructors teach in every week, a short drive east of our home base in Ajax. It is also home to the Oshawa DriveTest centre, which many Durham Region students choose for their road test. Whether you just passed your G1 knowledge test or you are getting ready for your G2 or G road test, this guide explains how lessons in Oshawa work, which local roads help you prepare and what it all costs.

## What does a driving lesson in Oshawa look like?

Every lesson is one-on-one, so the full hour is yours. Your instructor picks you up from your home, school or workplace in Oshawa, including the Durham College and Ontario Tech University area in the north end, and plans each lesson around what you need next.

Your first lesson usually includes:

- **Getting set up:** seat, mirrors and steering wheel position before you move.
- **A quick assessment:** starts, stops, turns and lane position on quiet streets.
- **Honest feedback:** what you already do well and which habits would cost marks on a road test.
- **A plan:** a realistic estimate of how many lessons you need and what each one will cover.

Each lesson after that builds on the last one, with extra time on the skills family practice often misses, such as complete stops, shoulder checks and smooth lane changes.

## What licence do you need to start lessons in Oshawa?

You need a **G1 licence**, which you get by passing a vision test and the written knowledge test at a DriveTest centre. Once you have it, you can start lessons with a certified driving instructor right away.

While you hold a G1 in Ontario, you must:

- Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
- Not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
- Not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways such as the 401.
- Always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience, and who is the only other person in the front seat.

There is one important exception. When you drive with a driving instructor certified in Ontario, you can drive on any road. For Oshawa drivers that matters, because Highway 401 runs along the south end of the city and Highway 407 crosses the north. A lesson is the only way to practise on them while you hold a G1.

## Local roads worth practising on in Oshawa

Oshawa has quiet subdivisions, a downtown with one-way streets, wide arterial roads and two highways, all within a few minutes of each other. Lessons usually move through them in stages:

| Stage | Type of road | What you practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Residential streets in neighbourhoods like Windfields, Samac and Eastdale | Steering control, full stops, speed control, scanning for pedestrians |
| Building up | Main roads such as Ritson Road, Harmony Road and Stevenson Road | Left turns at lights, lane changes, following distance |
| Busier traffic | Downtown Oshawa, Simcoe Street and Taunton Road | One-way streets, buses, plaza entrances and pedestrians |
| G test prep | Highway 401 and Highway 407 | Merging, exiting and changing lanes at highway speed |

DriveTest does not publish its test routes, so memorizing streets is not the goal. The goal is to feel comfortable on every type of road, so nothing on test day feels new.

## How many lessons will you need?

Ontario does not set a minimum number of lessons. It does set waiting periods: you must hold your G1 for 12 months before your G2 road test, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course. After that, you hold your G2 for 12 months before you can take the G road test.

As a rough guide for Oshawa students:

1. **Confident drivers** who practise often with family usually need 2 to 3 hours of test-focused lessons.
2. **Drivers who still hesitate** at left turns, lane changes or busy intersections usually need 4 to 5 hours.
3. **Complete beginners** do best with 10 hours or a full BDE course.

## Tips for getting the most out of your lessons

- **Practise between lessons.** Even 20 minutes on quiet streets helps new skills stick.
- **Get used to one-way streets.** Downtown Oshawa is a good place to practise lane choice and turning from the correct lane.
- **Ask questions.** Your instructor would rather explain a right of way rule twice than watch you guess on test day.
- **Know the common mistakes.** Read our list of [G2 road test automatic fails](/blog/g2-road-test-automatic-fails/), and for the G test, our [highway tips](/blog/g-road-test-highway-tips/).
