## What does a driving lesson in Pickering look like?

Every lesson is one-on-one. Your instructor picks you up from your home, school or workplace in Pickering, and the hour is planned around what you need next, not a fixed script.

A typical first lesson covers:

- **Setting up the car:** seat, mirrors and steering wheel before you move.
- **A short assessment:** starts, stops, turns and lane position on quiet streets.
- **Clear feedback:** what you already do well and the habits that would cost marks on a road test.
- **A plan:** a realistic estimate of how many lessons you need and what each one will cover.

After that, each lesson picks up where the last one ended. If you practise with family between lessons, your instructor will spend the time on the skills family practice tends to miss, like proper observation at intersections and smooth lane changes.

## What licence do you need to start lessons?

You need a **G1 licence**. You get it by passing the vision test and the written knowledge test at a DriveTest centre. Once you have it, you can drive with a certified driving instructor right away.

G1 drivers in Ontario must:

- Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
- Not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
- Not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways, unless the accompanying driver is a driving instructor certified in Ontario.
- Always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience.

The highway rule is a big one in Pickering. Highway 401 runs along the south end of the city and Highway 407 crosses the north, and the only way a G1 driver can practise on them is with a certified instructor.

## Local roads worth practising on in Pickering

Pickering has every kind of road you will meet on a road test, often within a few minutes of each other. Lessons usually move through them in stages:

| Stage | Type of road | What you practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Residential streets and crescents | Steering control, full stops, speed control, scanning for pedestrians |
| Building up | Main roads such as Liverpool Road, Whites Road and Brock Road | Left turns at lights, lane changes, following distance |
| Busier traffic | Kingston Road and Bayly Street | Multi-lane driving, plaza entrances, buses and turning traffic |

DriveTest does not publish its test routes, so memorizing streets is not the goal. The goal is to be comfortable on every type of road, so nothing on test day feels new.

## How many lessons will you need?

Ontario does not set a minimum number of lessons. What it does set is waiting time: you must hold your G1 for 12 months before your G2 road test, or 8 months if you complete a government-approved driver education course.

As a rough guide for Pickering students:

1. **Confident drivers** who practise often with family usually need 2 to 3 hours of test-focused lessons.
2. **Drivers who still hesitate** at left turns, lane changes or busy intersections usually need 4 to 5 hours.
3. **Complete beginners** do best with 10 hours or a full BDE course.

## Tips for getting the most out of your lessons

- **Practise between lessons.** Even 20 minutes on quiet streets with a family member helps new skills stick.
- **Ask about anything you are unsure of.** Your instructor would rather explain a right of way rule twice than watch you guess on test day.
- **Book your last lesson near your test centre.** It lets you see the area and settle your nerves.
- **Read the common mistakes.** Our list of [G2 road test automatic fails](/blog/g2-road-test-automatic-fails/) shows what examiners watch most closely.
