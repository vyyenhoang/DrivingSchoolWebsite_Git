How Many Driving Lessons Do I Need Before My G2 Test?

By Public Star Driving School · November 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Ontario does not set a minimum number of professional lessons for the G2 road test, but you must hold your G1 for 12 months first, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course. As a rule of thumb, drivers who already practise regularly with a family member usually need 2 to 5 hours with an instructor to fix habits and learn the test, while true beginners are better off with 10 hours or a full BDE course.

"How many lessons do I need?" is the first thing most new drivers ask us. The honest answer is that it depends on how much you have already driven, not on a number set by the government. This guide explains what Ontario actually requires, how to judge where you are, and how many hours make sense for different kinds of drivers in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa.

Does Ontario require a minimum number of driving lessons?

No. Ontario does not require you to take any professional lessons before your G2 road test (the G1 exit test). What it does require is time:

  • You must hold your G1 licence for 12 months before your first road test.
  • If you finish a government-approved driver education course, you can take the road test after 8 months.
  • You have five years to finish the whole graduated licensing process, or you start over.

So lessons are not a legal box to tick. They are there to get you ready to pass and to drive safely afterwards. If you are thinking about the 8 month option, our guide on whether the BDE course is worth it walks through the trade-offs, and our G1 to G licence timeline shows every step from G1 to your full G.

How many lessons do most drivers need?

There is no official statistic on this, so we will not invent one. What we can share is a simple guide based on where you are starting from:

Your situationSuggested lessons with an instructor
You drive several times a week with a family member and feel confident2 to 3 hours, focused on test skills
You practise sometimes but still hesitate at left turns or lane changes4 to 5 hours
You have barely driven, or you are very nervous10 hours, or a full BDE course
You failed a previous G2 test2 to 3 hours on the items on your scoresheet

These are starting points. Your instructor will tell you after the first lesson whether you need more or less.

Signs you are ready for the G2 road test

Hours matter less than skills. Before you book, you should be able to do all of these smoothly, without someone in the passenger seat reminding you:

  1. Full stops behind the line at every stop sign, then a clear look both ways.
  2. Mirror, signal, shoulder check before every lane change and every pull-out from the curb.
  3. Left turns at busy lights, waiting in the intersection for a safe gap.
  4. Parallel parking and a three-point turn on a quiet street.
  5. Residential driving at a steady speed, watching for pedestrians and parked cars.
  6. Keeping a safe following distance in traffic.

Ontario lists these skills on its G2 road test page: left and right turns, stopping, parallel parking, one-way streets, lane changes, three-point turns, residential neighbourhoods, and traffic lights, stop and yield signs. If any of them still feels shaky, that is where your lessons should go.

For a full test-day checklist, read how to pass your G2 road test on the first try.

Why practice with family is not always enough

Practising with a parent or friend is a great way to build hours behind the wheel. The catch is that family members often pass on habits that cost marks on a road test, such as rolling stops, skipped shoulder checks, or one hand on the wheel. They also may not know what examiners look for.

A few lessons with a certified instructor help you:

  • Spot and fix bad habits before they become automatic.
  • Learn the test format, including how the examiner gives directions.
  • Practise on roads like those near your test centre, since DriveTest does not publish its routes.
  • Avoid automatic fails, like the ones in our G2 road test automatic fails list.

One more benefit: Ontario lets a G1 driver with a certified driving instructor drive on any road, so lessons are also a chance to try roads you cannot use with a family member.

Should you book lessons all at once or spread them out?

Spread them out if you can. One or two lessons a week, with family practice in between, works better than five hours crammed into the week of your test. You get time to practise what the instructor taught you, then come back and fix what is still not right.

A good plan for most drivers looks like this:

  • First lesson: a full assessment, so you know exactly what to work on.
  • Middle lessons: focus on your weak spots, such as parking or busy intersections.
  • Last lesson: a mock road test, then a warm-up drive on test day.

Frequently asked questions

Is 2 hours of driving lessons enough for the G2 test?

It can be, if you already drive often and confidently with a family member. Two hours is usually enough to fix a few habits and run through the test. If you are still unsure about parking or busy intersections, book more.

Can I take the G2 test without any lessons?

Yes. Ontario does not require professional lessons. You only need to hold your G1 for 12 months, or 8 months with an approved driver education course.

How many lessons do I need if I have never driven before?

Start with an assessment lesson. Most complete beginners need around 10 hours with an instructor, plus regular practice, before they are ready. A BDE course is also a good fit and can shorten your G1 wait to 8 months.

Do lessons have to be with a certified instructor?

To count toward the 8 month option, you need to finish a government-approved driver education course. Ontario lists approved driving schools on ontario.ca. Practice with a fully licensed family member does not shorten the wait.

When should I take my last lesson?

Ideally within a few days of your test, followed by a warm-up drive on the day itself. That keeps the skills fresh without leaving you tired.
