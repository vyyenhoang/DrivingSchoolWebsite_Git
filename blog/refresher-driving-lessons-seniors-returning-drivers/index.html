Maybe you have not driven since you moved to Canada years ago. Maybe a spouse always did the driving, or you are heading into your 80+ licence renewal and want to feel sure of yourself. Whatever the reason, a few refresher lessons are one of the easiest ways to get comfortable behind the wheel again.

This guide covers who refresher lessons are for, what Ontario's senior renewal and expired licence rules ask of you, and what a lesson with us looks like.

Who are refresher driving lessons for?

Refresher lessons are for anyone who already knows how to drive but wants to feel safer or more confident. Common reasons include:

  • Seniors who want to check their habits before an 80+ renewal or after a health change
  • Returning drivers who stopped driving for a few years and need to rebuild confidence
  • Drivers whose licence expired and who now have to pass tests again
  • Newcomers who drove abroad and want to learn local rules (see our foreign licence exchange guide)
  • Nervous drivers after a collision, a long break or a move to a busier city

There is no test or grade in a refresher lesson. The goal is simply to drive with an instructor beside you, spot habits worth updating and practise the roads you actually use.

Ontario licence renewal for drivers 80 and over

According to ontario.ca, once you turn 80 you renew your driver's licence every 2 years instead of every 5. Here is how it works:

  1. Watch for your letter. A renewal form and a letter explaining the steps arrive in the mail up to 90 days before your licence expires.
  2. Prepare. Ontario asks you to watch its senior driver education video and review the Official MTO Driver's Handbook.
  3. Book an appointment at a ServiceOntario centre, online or by phone.
  4. Go to the appointment. You complete a vision assessment and a 5 minute screening exercise. Bring your licence, your renewal form, the glasses or contacts you use for driving and reading, and hearing aids if you use them.
  5. Pay the renewal fee. Ontario lists it as $36, and the new card arrives by mail in 4 to 6 weeks.

The screening exercise is a short paper task, not a computer test, and it does not depend on language. Ontario explains it as a clock drawing exercise that checks how you recognize and organize information, and translated instructions are available in many languages on the ontario.ca page.

After the session, some drivers are asked to pass a road test, see their doctor or send in more vision information. If that happens, you will be told by mail.

Should seniors take a lesson before an 80+ road test?

If you are asked to take a road test, a lesson or two beforehand is a smart way to prepare. An instructor can tell you what examiners watch for, such as full stops, shoulder checks and lane position, and let you practise on similar roads. Our guide on road test anxiety also has simple ways to stay calm on the day.

Even if no road test is required, many older drivers book a refresher to make sure their habits still match today's roads: more traffic, roundabouts, bike lanes and busier highways.

Renewing an expired licence in Ontario

It is illegal to drive with an expired licence in Ontario. What you need to renew depends on how long your full G licence has been expired, according to ontario.ca:

Expired forWhat you need
Less than 1 yearNo extra tests listed
1 to 3 yearsA vision test
3 to 10 yearsA vision test, a written knowledge test and two road tests
More than 10 yearsRe-apply as a new driver and serve all graduated licensing waiting periods

If your licence has been expired 3 to 10 years, you can book your first road test as soon as you pass the written and vision tests, and the second as soon as you pass the first. That is where refresher lessons help the most: you are being tested to the same standard as new drivers. Our G2 road test guide and G road test highway tips explain what each test covers.

The renewal fee is $90 plus any test fees. Check drivetest.ca for current written and road test fees before you book. Learner licences (G1 and G2) cannot be renewed once they expire, so contact DriveTest if yours is close to its expiry date.

What happens in a refresher lesson?

Every refresher lesson is shaped around you. A typical first lesson looks like this:

  1. A short chat about how long it has been, what worries you and where you want to drive.
  2. Quiet streets first so you can get used to the car, mirrors and controls.
  3. A habit check on stops, shoulder checks, signals, speed and following distance.
  4. Real routes, such as your grocery store, your doctor's office or a highway merge, once you feel ready.
  5. A plan for what to practise next, with honest feedback and no pressure.

Many drivers also like to review defensive driving tips between lessons, and our winter driving tips are useful if you are getting back on the road this season.

How many refresher lessons do you need?

It depends on how long you have been off the road and how comfortable you feel. Some drivers only want one lesson to check their habits. Others who have not driven in many years prefer several lessons spread over a few weeks. Your instructor will give you an honest view after the first lesson, and you can decide from there.

Frequently asked questions

How often do seniors renew their licence in Ontario?

Drivers under 80 renew every 5 years. Once you turn 80, you renew every 2 years, with a vision assessment and a 5 minute screening exercise at a ServiceOntario appointment.

Do seniors have to take a road test in Ontario?

Not always. After the renewal session, some drivers 80 and over are asked to pass a road test or provide medical or vision information. You will be notified by mail if that applies to you.

My licence expired 5 years ago. What do I need to do?

For a full G licence expired 3 to 10 years, ontario.ca says you need a vision test, a written knowledge test and two road tests. You visit a DriveTest centre, bring original ID and pay the $90 renewal fee plus test fees.

Can I take refresher lessons in my own car?

Our lessons use our dual-brake training car, which is safer while you rebuild confidence. Call us if you have a specific question about your situation.

Are refresher lessons only for seniors?

No. They suit anyone who has not driven in a while, is nervous after a break or collision, or is new to driving in Ontario.
