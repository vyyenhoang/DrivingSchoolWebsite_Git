G2 vs G Road Test: What Is the Difference in Ontario?

By Public Star Driving School · November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The G2 road test (the G1 exit test) checks basic skills like turns, stops, parallel parking, three-point turns and residential driving, and you can take it after 12 months with your G1, or 8 months with an approved BDE course. The G road test (the G2 exit test) comes at least 12 months later and focuses on advanced driving: merging on and off expressways, lane changes at speed, intersections and business areas. The G test currently leaves out parallel parking, the roadside stop, the three-point turn and residential driving.

Ontario has two road tests on the way to a full licence, and people often mix them up. The G2 test is about proving you can control a car safely on everyday streets. The G test is about proving you can handle faster, busier traffic on your own. Knowing what each one checks helps you practise the right skills at the right time.

The two road tests at a glance

Ontario's graduated licensing system has two levels. You pass the first road test to move from G1 to G2, and the second road test to move from G2 to a full G licence.

When you can take each test

G2 test: you can take your first road test after 12 months with a G1 licence. If you complete an approved Beginner Driver Education (BDE) course, that drops to 8 months. Our guide on whether the BDE course is worth it explains the trade-offs.

G test: you can take it after 12 months with your G2 licence.

Ontario gives you up to five years to finish the whole process. If you do not get your full G in that time, you have to start over. That means the fastest path from a new G1 to a full G is at least 20 months.

If you fail either test, you must wait at least 10 days before you can take it again.

What the G2 road test checks

According to ontario.ca, the G2 test covers the core skills every driver needs:

  • Turning left and right
  • Stopping
  • Parallel parking
  • One-way streets
  • Lane changes
  • Three-point turns
  • Driving in residential neighbourhoods
  • Traffic lights, stop signs and yield signs

The G2 test is slower paced, but it is very detailed. Examiners watch for full stops behind the line, proper shoulder checks, safe gaps before left turns and smooth control of the car. Small habits such as rolling a stop or forgetting to check your blind spot add up quickly. Our G2 road test guide walks through each manoeuvre and the common mistakes.

What the G road test checks

The G test covers more advanced skills. Ontario lists:

  • Driving on major roads and expressways, including merging on and off
  • Turns, curves and lane changes
  • Intersections
  • Driving in business areas

Ontario currently says the standard G test does not include parallel parking, roadside stops, three-point turns or driving in residential neighbourhoods, because those were already covered on the G2 test. Rules can change, so check ontario.ca before your test.

With the slow manoeuvres removed, nearly all of your result comes from how you drive in moving traffic. Speed control, mirror checks, safe following distance and confident merging matter most. Our G road test highway checklist breaks this down step by step.

The highway declaration: only for the G test

Before the G test begins, you must complete a Declaration of Highway Driving Experience. It describes the driving you have done on highways with speed limits of at least 80 km/h.

If you do not have enough highway experience, the examiner will declare the test "out of order." You lose 50 per cent of your road test fee, and the other half stays as a credit on your record. To rebook, you pay the half that was deducted. Plan regular highway trips in the months before your G test so this never happens.

There is no highway declaration for the G2 test. G1 drivers are not allowed on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways with a posted limit over 80 km/h, unless they are with a certified driving instructor.

How the restrictions change between levels

Each licence level comes with its own rules. Here is a short summary from the MTO driver's handbook:

G1 drivers must:

  • Have zero blood alcohol
  • Drive with a fully licensed driver with at least four years of experience in the front passenger seat
  • Not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
  • Stay off 400-series highways and high-speed expressways (unless with a certified driving instructor)

G2 drivers must:

  • Have zero blood alcohol
  • Have a working seatbelt for every passenger
  • If 19 or under, carry only one passenger aged 19 or under between midnight and 5 a.m. for the first six months, then up to three (exceptions apply if a fully licensed driver is in the front seat or passengers are immediate family)

All drivers aged 21 and under must have zero blood alcohol, whatever their licence class.

Which test is harder?

It depends on the driver. Students who are calm and precise in quiet areas often find the G2 test comfortable but feel nervous on the 401. Students who drive to work on the highway every day sometimes find the G test easy but slip on G2 details like parallel parking.

A better question is which skills you are weakest at. If merging into fast traffic makes you tense, focus on highway practice before your G test. If parking and three-point turns are shaky, fix those before your G2.

Fees and booking

You prepay the road test fee when you book. The current G2 and G road test fees are listed on drivetest.ca. To avoid losing your fee, cancel or reschedule at least 48 hours before your appointment. MTO and DriveTest do not charge a fee just to book a road test, so be careful with any site that asks for one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the G test harder than the G2 test?

The G test is faster and includes highway driving, while the G2 test has more slow manoeuvres like parallel parking. Which one feels harder depends on your weaker skills.

Do you parallel park on the G road test?

Not currently. Ontario says the standard G test does not include parallel parking, roadside stops, three-point turns or residential driving, because those are covered on the G2 test.

How long after my G2 can I take the G test?

You need to hold your G2 licence for 12 months. You have five years in total to finish the whole licensing process.

Can I skip the G2 test and go straight to G?

No. You must pass the G2 road test first, then hold your G2 licence for at least 12 months before you can take the G road test.
