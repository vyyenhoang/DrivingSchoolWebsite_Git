Ontario G1 to G Licence Timeline and Costs: How Long It Takes

By Public Star Driving School · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

In Ontario you hold your G1 for 12 months, or 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course, then hold your G2 for 12 months before the G road test. That makes the minimum 20 months with a course or 24 months without, and Ontario says most people take about 20 months. You have up to five years to finish, or you start over. Lessons are optional, and with us G2 lessons start at $50 + HST ($56.50) an hour, while DriveTest's own test and licence fees are listed on drivetest.ca.

Getting a full G licence in Ontario is a two-step process, and the waiting periods matter more than anything else when you plan it. This guide lays out the timeline from your first G1 to your full G, what you can and cannot do at each stage, and what the process can cost. It is written for new drivers in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa, but the rules are the same everywhere in Ontario.

How long does it take to get a G licence in Ontario?

Ontario says that for most people the two-step process takes about 20 months. Here is how that breaks down, based on the rules on ontario.ca:

StageMinimum timeHow you move on
G112 months, or 8 months with an approved driver education coursePass the G2 road test (the G1 exit test)
G212 monthsPass the G road test (the G2 exit test)
Whole process20 months with a course, 24 withoutYou must finish within 5 years

So the fastest route is 8 months on a G1 with a government-approved course, then 12 months on a G2: 20 months in total. Without a course, the minimum is 12 plus 12, so plan on at least 24 months from G1 to G.

The five year limit is the one people forget. Ontario says you have up to five years to finish the whole process, and if you do not get your full G licence in that time, you need to start over. If your G2 is getting close to that date, book your G test early so a cancellation or a failed attempt does not push you past it.

Step 1: getting your G1

You can apply for a driver's licence in Ontario once you are at least 16 years old. You pass a vision test and a knowledge test on the rules of the road and traffic signs at a DriveTest centre, and you pay a fee to apply and take the knowledge test. DriveTest keeps the current amounts on its list of fees, so check there before you go.

To study, use the official Driver's Handbook. Ontario lists the printed handbook at $14.95 plus taxes.

G1 rules you have to follow

Ontario's G1 conditions are strict, and breaking them can cost you your licence:

  • Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
  • Do not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
  • Do not drive on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways, unless you are with a driving instructor certified in Ontario.
  • Always drive with a fully licensed driver who has at least four years of driving experience and a blood alcohol level under .05 (zero if they are 21 or under). They must be the only other person in the front seat.
  • Make sure every passenger wears a working seatbelt.

Should you take a BDE course to shorten the G1 wait?

If you finish a government-approved driver education course, Ontario lets you take your first road test after 8 months instead of 12. That four month head start is the main reason many students choose a Beginner Driver Education (BDE) course, but the training itself matters just as much: you learn defensive driving and test skills from the start instead of picking up habits you later have to unlearn.

Step 2: the G2 road test and G2 rules

Once your G1 time is up, you can book the G2 road test. It checks basic driving skills: turns, stops, lane changes, parking and reversing. Our guide on how to pass the G2 road test on the first try covers what examiners look for, and how many driving lessons you need before your G2 test helps you decide how much help to get.

After you pass, you hold a G2 for 12 months. Ontario says G2 drivers can drive on all Ontario roads, at any time of day in most cases, without another experienced driver in the car. A few rules still apply:

  • Keep a zero blood alcohol level.
  • Carry only as many passengers as there are working seatbelts.
  • If you are 19 or under, between midnight and 5 a.m. you can only carry one passenger aged 19 or under during your first six months on a G2, and up to three after that. These limits do not apply if a fully licensed driver with at least four years of experience is in the front seat, or if your passengers are immediate family. Our guide to G2 licence rules and restrictions covers every G2 condition and the penalties for breaking them.

Step 3: the G road test

After 12 months on your G2, you can take the G road test. This test is longer and includes highway or expressway driving, so it is worth getting real practice at highway speeds before you book. See our G road test highway tips and the G2 vs G road test comparison to see what changes.

What does it cost to go from G1 to G?

The total depends on how much help you want. There are two kinds of costs:

  1. DriveTest fees, which the government sets: the knowledge test and licence, and each road test. These are the same at every DriveTest centre. We do not repeat the amounts here because they change, so check the current DriveTest fee list.
  2. Optional training and a test car, which you choose. Ontario does not require any paid lessons.

Tips for keeping your timeline on track

  • Book road tests early. Test dates near Scarborough and Durham can fill up, so book as soon as you know when your waiting period ends.
  • Count from your licence date. Your 8 or 12 months start when you get the licence, not when you start practising.
  • Watch the five year limit. Write down the date your G1 was issued and plan backward from the five year mark.
  • New to Canada? If you already hold a licence from another country, read our guide to converting a foreign driver's licence in Ontario first. Your experience may shorten your path.
  • Practise between lessons. Waiting periods only help if you actually drive. Regular practice with a licensed family member makes every lesson count more.

Frequently asked questions

How long do you have to wait between G1 and G2 in Ontario?

Ontario says you can take your first road test after 12 months with a G1, or after 8 months if you finish a government-approved driver education course.

How long do you have to wait between G2 and G?

You can take the G road test after 12 months with a G2 licence.

How long do I have to get my full G licence?

Up to five years from the start of the process. If you do not get your full G in that time, you need to start over.

Can I drive on the highway with a G1?

Not on 400-series highways or high-speed expressways, unless you are driving with a driving instructor certified in Ontario. G2 drivers can drive on all Ontario roads.

How much are the DriveTest fees?

DriveTest sets the fees for the knowledge test, licence and road tests, and they can change. Check the current amounts on drivetest.ca before you book.
