How to Lower Car Insurance as a New Driver in Ontario: 9 Tips That Work

By Public Star Driving School · November 25, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

To lower car insurance as a new driver in Ontario, shop around every year, finish a driver training course your insurer recognizes, start as a named occasional driver on a parent's or guardian's policy instead of a principal driver, and keep a clean record. Ontario's regulator, FSRA, says new drivers with no chargeable convictions or at-fault accidents should get a 10 percent rate reduction for one year when they move to a G2, and another 10 percent for one year when they get a full G. All insurers in Ontario must also offer a winter tire discount.

Car insurance is often the biggest surprise for new drivers. You pass your G1 test, start practising, and then a quote arrives that costs more than you expected. The good news is that you have more control over your premium than you might think. This guide explains how insurance works for new drivers in Ontario and nine practical ways to pay less, using information from Ontario's insurance regulator.

It is written for new drivers and parents in Scarborough, North York, Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa, but the rules apply across Ontario. We are a driving school, not an insurance company, so always confirm discounts and prices with your own broker, agent or insurer.

Do you need car insurance as a new driver in Ontario?

Yes. Ontario has compulsory auto insurance, which means every vehicle registered in the province must be insured, and you must show proof of insurance to register a vehicle or renew its registration. The Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA) regulates auto insurance in the province.

Driving without valid insurance is expensive. According to FSRA, fines for owners, lessees and drivers without valid auto insurance range from $5,000 to $50,000, your licence can be suspended and your vehicle impounded. A conviction can also mark you as a high-risk driver, which means much higher premiums later.

Why is insurance so expensive for new drivers?

FSRA says your price depends on the insurance company you choose and your risk characteristics, including:

  • how many years you have been licensed,
  • how many years you have been accident-free,
  • the vehicle you drive,
  • where you live.

A new driver has no years of experience and no claims history yet, so insurers have little to go on. That is why the first few years usually cost the most, and why every clean year on your record helps.

9 ways to lower car insurance as a new driver

1. Shop around every year

FSRA is clear on this one: do not assume companies charge the same price for the same coverage. Rates change over time, so the company with the best price last year may not be the best value now. Get several quotes, and compare them at renewal time rather than switching in the middle of a policy year, which can bring cancellation penalties.

2. Take a recognized driver training course

FSRA says most insurance companies offer a discount or reduced premium for new drivers who complete a recognized driver training program. In Ontario, that usually means a Ministry-approved Beginner Driver Education (BDE) course. Before you sign up, ask your insurer whether they recognize the course and how much it changes your quote. Our guide on whether the BDE course is worth it also explains how BDE lets you take your G2 road test after 8 months instead of 12.

3. Use the graduated licence discount

This is one many new drivers do not know about. FSRA says drivers moving through the graduated licensing system should receive a 10 percent rate reduction on all coverages for one year when they move to a G2, as long as they have no chargeable convictions or at-fault accidents. You should get another 10 percent for one year when you get your full G, again with a clean record during your G2. If your premium did not drop after a road test, call your insurer and ask.

4. Start as a named occasional driver

FSRA suggests young drivers consider gaining experience as a named occasional driver on a parent's or guardian's policy, rather than as the principal driver of their own vehicle. It says premiums for young occasional drivers are much lower than for young principal drivers. Be honest with your insurer about who drives the car most: listing the wrong principal driver can cause serious problems with a claim.

5. Keep a clean driving record

FSRA calls a record free of at-fault accidents and convictions the best way to get low premiums in the future. Tickets matter twice for new drivers: they raise your insurance, and they can also lead to suspensions under Ontario's novice rules. Our guides to G2 licence rules and demerit points for G1 and G2 drivers explain how quickly a novice can lose their licence.

6. Install winter tires

All insurance companies in Ontario must offer a discount if you put winter tires on your vehicle. Ask your insurer how to qualify. Winter tires are also one of the best safety upgrades for a new driver, as our winter driving tips for new drivers explain.

7. Choose the car carefully

FSRA notes that a vehicle with a high theft rate costs more to insure, and that good security features can help. Before you buy, get insurance quotes on the exact models you are considering. A car that looks cheaper at the dealership can cost more once insurance is added.

8. Look at deductibles and coverage

A higher deductible means you pay more of a loss yourself, but it can lower your premium. FSRA also says optional collision or comprehensive coverage may not be worth it on a vehicle worth less than $2,000. On the other hand, it suggests thinking about raising your third-party liability above the $200,000 standard to $1 million or $2 million. Talk this through with your broker or agent before you cut anything.

9. Ask about every discount

Discounts vary by company, so ask each year. FSRA lists these examples:

DiscountWhat it is
Driver trainingMost companies offer one for new drivers who finish a recognized course
Graduated licence10 percent for one year at G2 and again at G, with a clean record
Winter tiresAll Ontario insurers must offer one
Multi-vehicleUsually 5 to 15 percent for insuring more than one vehicle with the same company
Multi-policyUsually 5 to 15 percent for buying car and home insurance together
StudentSome companies reward good grades or students who live away from home part of the year
Group ratesSome employers, unions and associations have group plans
Low mileage or alarmSome insurers lower premiums for low yearly distance or a vehicle alarm

Also pay your premium on time. FSRA warns that having a policy cancelled for non-payment can make insurers treat you as higher risk.

Frequently asked questions

Does the BDE course lower insurance in Ontario?

It can. FSRA says most insurers offer a discount to new drivers who complete a recognized driver training program. Ask your insurer for a quote with and without your BDE certificate.

Do I get an insurance discount after passing my G2?

You should. FSRA says drivers entering G2 with no chargeable convictions or at-fault accidents should get a 10 percent reduction on all coverages for one year, and another 10 percent for one year when they get their full G.

Is it cheaper to be added to my parents' insurance?

Often, yes. FSRA says premiums for young occasional drivers are much lower than for young principal drivers. You must be honest about who drives the car most.

Do winter tires lower car insurance in Ontario?

Yes. All insurance companies in Ontario must offer a discount for winter tires. Ask your insurer how to qualify.

What happens if I drive without insurance in Ontario?

Fines range from $5,000 to $50,000, your licence can be suspended and your vehicle impounded, and insurers may treat you as a high-risk driver afterwards.
